# Film Business, Festivals & Funding

The business side a working filmmaker actually needs — how films get money, how festivals and sales work, how rights and deals are structured, and the economic realities that shape what gets made. Craft decides if a film is good; this decides if it exists and reaches anyone. Numbers here are rules of thumb, current-as-of-writing — verify specifics (incentive %, window lengths, AMPAS rules, fees) against the primary source before relying on them.

---

## 1. Financing — how the money is assembled

Indie films are almost never funded from one source. The producer stacks a **cap table** of instruments, each with different risk, cost, and payback priority, and "closes the gap" between what's committed and the budget.

- **Development money** — earliest, riskiest cash: option fees, script drafts, legal/chain-of-title, a sizzle. From the producer, a studio/streamer development deal, or funds/labs (Sundance Labs, Torino FilmLab). Mostly lost, since most developed projects die in "turnaround."
- **Equity** — investors buy a share of profit. Highest risk, so it sits near the *bottom* of the recoupment stack. Typically negotiates a **preferred return** (recoup 100–120% of capital before profit splits) plus a share of net, often **50/50** between the equity side and the producer/talent side after recoupment.
- **Pre-sales** — selling distribution rights *territory by territory* before the film is made, off the package (script + cast + director). A foreign distributor signs a contract promising a **Minimum Guarantee (MG)** on delivery; the producer takes those contracts to a bank, which **discounts** them (lends against them minus interest) for production cash. The classic engine of independent film. Value is driven almost entirely by the **cast's ask price** per territory.
- **Gap financing** — a bank loan covering the "gap" between money raised and the full budget, secured against the *unsold* territories' estimated value. Usually capped at ~10–20% of budget; expensive.
- **Soft money (incentives/rebates/grants)** — non-recoupable or cheap government money to attract production: cash rebates on local spend, **transferable/refundable tax credits** (Georgia, Louisiana — sold for cash), co-production treaties unlocking national funds. Often the *single largest source* — can cover 20–40%+ of a budget. Know: UK BFI/HMRC Audio-Visual Expenditure Credit, Ireland Section 481, Eurimages, Creative Europe MEDIA, France's CNC (the model everyone envies — a levy on tickets/broadcasters funds new work), Telefilm Canada, Screen Australia, German DFFF.
- **Grants (non-recoupable, no equity given up)** — dominate documentary and arthouse: Sundance Institute, ITVS, Ford/JustFilms, Chicken & Egg; broadcaster commissions (ARTE, ZDF, BBC Film, Film4) double as funding; the national funds above.
- **Crowdfunding** — Kickstarter (all-or-nothing) / Indiegogo (flexible). Realistic for finishing funds, shorts, docs, and *audience-building* — not for financing a $2M feature; the outliers (Veronica Mars) are anomalies. Reward-based gives up no equity; equity crowdfunding (Reg CF) does and is regulated.
- **Negative pickup** — a distributor contracts in advance to buy the finished film on delivery to spec; the producer borrows the budget against that contract.
- **Completion bond / completion guarantee** — insurance (Film Finances et al.) that the film will be finished and delivered on time/budget, or the guarantor steps in with cash and takeover rights. Required by banks and equity above micro-budget; costs ~1–3% of budget.

### Budget tiers (US-centric, rough)
- **Micro** — under ~$250k (often <$50k): no-name cast, deferrals, favors. SAG Ultra-Low/Micro agreements. Exit = festival premiere → small acquisition or self-distribution.
- **Low / indie** — ~$250k–$5M: a name or two for pre-sales value. The classic Sundance zone.
- **Mid / upper-indie** — ~$5M–$20M: real names, sales-agent-driven, pre-sales + soft money + equity + gap. The **hollowed-out "missing middle."**
- **Studio** — ~$20M–$100M+ (mid-major) to $150–300M+ tentpole *negative cost*, plus marketing; balance-sheet or slate-financed.

### The recoupment waterfall (order of payback)
Each tier is fully paid before the next sees a cent:
1. **Exhibitor's cut, then distributor fee + P&A** (marketing, often recouped with interest).
2. **Sales agent's commission** (10–25% of gross) + capped expenses.
3. **Senior debt** — the bank (gap/pre-sale/negative-pickup loans) + interest.
4. **Deferrals** — deferred fees to cast/crew/vendors.
5. **Equity preferred return** — 100–120% of capital back.
6. **Net profit split** — commonly 50/50 producer-side vs. financier-side, with talent back-end carved out.

Key reality: so much comes off the top that **equity and net-profit points are frequently worth zero**. This is why smart money leans on soft money and pre-sales (de-risked before a foot is shot) over pure equity speculation.

## 2. Festivals

**What they're actually for:** a market launchpad (buyers attend), a press/review record that drives acquisition price, prestige/awards positioning, distribution (including Oscar-qualifying runs), and filmmaker discovery. Different films want different things — a starry mid-budget wants a *sale*; an auteur wants *prestige*; a doc wants *impact + broadcaster pickup*.

**The tiers.**
- **The "Big Three" A-list (Europe):** **Cannes** (May — apex of world-cinema prestige; the biggest market, the Marché du Film; Palme d'Or; demands world premieres for Competition; parallel sections Directors' Fortnight, Critics' Week), **Venice** (Aug/Sep — oldest, Golden Lion, now the **fall Oscar-season launchpad**), **Berlinale** (Feb — Golden Bear, politically engaged, big market: the European Film Market/EFM).
- **Major North American:** **Sundance** (Jan — the premier US *independent* festival and biggest *acquisition* market), **TIFF/Toronto** (Sep — huge, the People's Choice Award is a strong Oscar bellwether), **Telluride** (Labor Day — small, secret, no announced lineup, premiere-flexible, pure Oscar-tastemaker), **SXSW** (Mar — genre/comedy/discovery), **Tribeca** (Jun).
- **Other key international:** Rotterdam/IFFR (experimental; CineMart co-pro market), Busan/BIFF (the key Asian festival + Asian Project Market), San Sebastián, Locarno, Karlovy Vary; docs: IDFA, Sheffield DocFest, Hot Docs; shorts: Clermont-Ferrand.
- **FIAPF** formally accredits/categorizes festivals, but industry reputation matters more than FIAPF tier.

**The Oscar-qualifying circuit.** Eligibility needs a paid **theatrical run** meeting AMPAS rules (historically 7 consecutive days in a qualifying market, now broadened to multiple US markets). **Shorts and doc shorts** can qualify by *winning a qualifying award* at an accredited festival — which is why the Oscar-qualifying short circuit matters enormously. International Feature = one official submission per country. Rules change yearly (oscars.org).

**Premiere-status politics.** Festivals gate slots by exclusivity: **world premiere** (first anywhere — required by Cannes Competition), **international** (first outside country of origin), **national**, **regional** (first in North America/Asia). You *spend* your world premiere once, on the highest-value festival you can realistically get; premiering too early or too small can burn a film's whole launch and disqualify it from a top festival.

**Strategy.** Aim high and protect status (submit to top festivals first, hold the world premiere until you hear back); match the film to a festival's identity; sequence world → international → the broader circuit → Oscar-qualifying run; involve a sales agent who shapes the run around the market; have deliverables/press kit ready.

**Submission realities.** **FilmFreeway** is the dominant platform. Fees ~$25–$120 per festival (higher late); a serious run costs hundreds to a few thousand dollars; waivers exist. Acceptance is brutal — Sundance gets ~15,000+ submissions for ~110–120 feature slots (well under 1%). Most top-festival slots come via **industry relationships / sales agents / programmer outreach**, not the cold submission pile.

## 3. Distribution & sales

**Sales agent vs. distributor** — the key distinction:
- A **sales agent** (world sales) represents the *producer*, sells the film to *distributors* territory by territory (and to streamers), takes **10–25%** commission plus capped expenses, and works the markets. Does not release to the public. (Protagonist, Cornerstone, mk2, HanWay, The Match Factory, XYZ for genre, Charades…)
- A **distributor** buys rights *in a territory* and actually *releases* the film — theatrical booking, marketing, home/VOD/TV. (A24, Neon, IFC, Magnolia, Sony Pictures Classics; studios for wide releases.)
A film usually has many distributors (one per territory) under one sales agent; an "all-rights" worldwide deal (increasingly a streamer) collapses this into one buyer.

**Windows and their collapse.** The traditional staggered sequence — **theatrical** (once ~90 days exclusive) → **TVOD/EST** (rent/buy) → **PVOD** (premium early rental ~$20–30) → **Pay-1/SVOD** (subscription: Netflix, Max, Disney+) → **AVOD/FAST** (ad-supported: Tubi, Pluto, Roku) → free TV. Acronyms: **SVOD** subscription, **TVOD** transactional/pay-per-title, **AVOD** ad-supported, **EST** electronic sell-through (own a digital copy), **PVOD** premium early VOD, **FAST** free ad-supported streaming TV. The 90-day theatrical exclusive has eroded to **~17–45 days** (or day-and-date, or streaming-only) — driven by COVID, streamer economics, and the death of the DVD sell-through window. For indies, theatrical is now often a **marketing loss-leader** before the real money window (streaming/VOD).

**Minimum Guarantee (MG)** — the upfront amount a distributor/streamer commits, recoupable against their revenue share (an advance against royalties). A streamer **buyout** = a flat fee for all rights with **no back-end** — often cost-plus (budget + ~10–30% margin) but forfeiting all long-tail upside. Streamers de-risk but cap upside.

**The markets** (where sales agents sell): **Marché du Film** (Cannes — the world's largest), **EFM** (Berlin), **AFM/American Film Market** (Nov). Also Ventana Sur (Latin America), Hong Kong FILMART (Asia), MIPCOM/MIPTV (TV). Pre-sales happen here off a one-sheet before shooting.

**Four-quadrant thinking.** Hollywood segments audiences into four quadrants (male/female × under/over 25). A **four-quadrant film** (broad blockbuster, family, event) targets all four and justifies the biggest marketing spend; most films are two-quadrant (action skews male, rom-com female) or one-quadrant niche.

**P&A (Prints & Advertising)** — the marketing + distribution cost of a release (advertising, publicity, trailers, once physical prints). *Separate* from the production/negative budget, often separately financed, and for a wide release **frequently equals or exceeds the production budget**. Recouped early in the waterfall (often with interest). Insufficient P&A is why good indie films die unseen.

## 4. Deals & rights

- **Option vs. purchase.** An **option** *rents the exclusive right to buy* an underlying property (novel, article, life story) for a fixed period (commonly 12–18 months, renewable); the fee is often ~10% of the eventual purchase price (or nominal for indies). You don't own it during the option — you have the exclusive right to set it up and later **exercise** the purchase (the price is negotiated up front). Options are cheap risk control: tie up material without buying until the project is real.
- **Life rights.** No single "life rights" property exists in US law (you can't copyright a life), but a **life-rights agreement** secures the subject's *cooperation* (access, private material), a *release* against defamation/false-light/right-of-publicity claims, and sometimes exclusivity. You *can* make an unauthorized film about public figures/events, but life rights de-risk and add authenticity.
- **Guilds.** **WGA** (writers) governs minimums, arbitrates *credit* ("Written by"/"Screenplay by"/"Story by"), reserves *separated rights* to original-material writers, and sets *residuals*. **DGA** (directors), **SAG-AFTRA** (actors — with the low-budget agreement tiers that let indies afford union talent), **IATSE** (crew), **Teamsters**. Streaming residuals and AI provisions were the core of the 2023 WGA/SAG-AFTRA strikes.
- **Back-end / points — gross vs. net.** **Gross points** (a share of defined gross, before most deductions) are rare, hugely valuable, and reserved for A-list leverage — where the real money is. **Net points** ("net profit participation") come after the studio deducts distribution fees, P&A, interest, and overhead; because of **Hollywood accounting** even massive hits famously "never show a net profit" (*Forrest Gump* "lost money"), so net points are nicknamed **"monkey points."** Rule: **gross is real, net is theater.** Sophisticated deals negotiate the *definition*.
- **Chain of title** — the unbroken documented trail proving the producer controls every right needed to make and exploit the film: underlying rights, writer agreements, copyright registration, title report, and **E&O (Errors & Omissions) insurance**. No distributor, bank, bond, or streamer closes without clean chain of title; a single gap (an unsigned writer, an uncleared song) can kill a deal at delivery.
- **Final cut authority** — *the* creative power question of a film deal: who controls the final version. **Final cut** (the contractual right to lock the theatrical cut) is rare and reserved for proven directors with leverage; most directors instead get the **DGA-guaranteed director's cut** (at least one cut before the studio takes over), after which the studio holds final say and can recut. Spelled out in the deal alongside budget and schedule; the fight over the cut (test-screening-driven recuts, "studio cut" vs. "director's cut") is where many films are won or lost.

## 5. The pitch & packaging

A **package** = the bundle that makes a project financeable: **script/rights + director + key cast + a producer + a piece of finance or a sales estimate.** Financiers don't fund an idea — they fund a package whose elements de-risk the bet.

**Why talent attachment matters.** Cast drives pre-sale value (sales agents keep territory-by-territory value grids per actor); a director signals vision and, for auteurs, festival prestige. The **chicken-and-egg problem** — talent won't commit without money, money won't commit without talent — is broken with "if/come" or "pay-or-play" offers and letters of intent (LOIs). **Casting is financing.**

**The machinery.** Talent agencies (**CAA, WME, UTA**) package their own clients and take ~10% (agency "packaging fees" were curtailed after the 2019–21 WGA–agency fight). Managers develop careers and often attach as producers. Entertainment lawyers paper the deals and broker introductions, especially in indie.

**Greenlight.** *Studio path:* development → creative execs → a **greenlight committee** weighs package, budget, comps, P&A, projected multi-window revenue, and quadrant appeal — a financial model of projected worldwide revenue vs. negative cost + P&A + overhead. *Indie path:* assemble rights + director + cast → sales agent issues estimates → stack pre-sales + soft money + equity + gap → completion bond + bank close → **"the money is closed" = greenlit.**

## 6. Key realities / numbers (rules of thumb)

- **Marketing ≈ production budget.** Global P&A for a wide release roughly equals the negative cost; true break-even is roughly **2–2.5× the production budget** in revenue.
- **Theatrical splits with exhibitors.** Box office is split between theater and distributor ("film rental"). US distributor share runs high early (~60–70%, sometimes with a theater "house nut" floor), declining over the run; blended domestic ~50%, international often ~40%. So a "$50M opening" doesn't mean the studio got $50M — it saw roughly half, *before* recouping P&A.
- **Streamer buyout economics.** Netflix/Apple/Amazon often buy on **cost-plus** (budget + ~10–30% margin), a full buyout with **no back-end and no box-office upside** — certainty vs. ceiling. Talent increasingly negotiates streaming success-bonuses to claw some upside back.
- **Most indie films lose money.** Widely cited: only **~a third or fewer** of independent films recoup; a small number of hits carry the whole slate (a power-law "hits business"). Causes: no distribution (many films never get a real release), tiny/no P&A (no awareness), a crowded market, and equity last in the waterfall. Investors are told to expect to lose their money; the upside is the rare breakout.
- **The "missing middle."** The $5–60M adult drama (the specialty sweet spot) has been hollowed out — studios chase four-quadrant IP tentpoles, streamers absorbed the mid-budget — squeezing indies between micro-budget and studio. *(Budget-tier bands are rules of thumb and overlap: "mid-budget" spans roughly $5–60M depending on who's counting — the sales-agent-driven upper-indie zone tops out around $20M, studio "mid-major" pictures run to ~$60M+. Treat the seams as fuzzy.)*
- **Test screenings & reshoots drive the final cut.** Studio films are shaped after "picture lock" by **recruited/preview test screenings** — an audience scores the film (the **NRG** recruited-screening process; **CinemaScore** measures opening-night audiences) and fills cards, and the reactions frequently *drive* recuts, new endings, and **reshoots / "additional photography"** (now a routine line item, not a crisis signal). This is the back half of "the edit is the final rewrite" — the audience-preview loop is often what *causes* the rewrite, and who controls the response to it is the final-cut question above.
