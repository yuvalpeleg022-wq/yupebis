# How the World's Top Filmmakers Think and Work

Grounded in the cited books (Mamet's *On Directing Film*, Murch's *In the Blink of an Eye*, Tarkovsky's *Sculpting in Time*), plus interviews and craft press. Each profile: **worldview → method on set → spottable signature → defining films → the one lesson to steal.**

## I. The Founders (Classical Masters)

### Alfred Hitchcock
- **Obsession:** Fear and guilt as universal audience emotions; the audience is the instrument he plays. Cinema is manipulation of *where the viewer's knowledge sits relative to the characters'.*
- **Method:** The film was finished in his head before shooting — he storyboarded exhaustively and found the actual shoot "boring." Called actors "cattle" (half-joke); he wanted precise placement, not improvisation. Total pre-visualization = total control.
- **Signature:** "Pure cinema" — meaning built by cutting, not dialogue (his gloss on the Kuleshov effect: same face, different meaning depending on the shot before it). POV/reaction-shot triads. Suspense = give the audience the bomb under the table the characters can't see. **This is *dramatic-irony* suspense (the audience knows more) — Hitchcock's favored mode, but not the only one: mystery/whodunit suspense runs the opposite way, on the audience knowing *less* than it wants (see `genre-playbooks.md`).** Vertigo dolly-zoom, voyeurism.
- **Films:** *Vertigo*, *Psycho*, *Rear Window*, *North by Northwest*, *Notorious*.
- **Steal:** Control information flow, not plot. Who knows what, when, is the entire engine of tension.

### Akira Kurosawa
- **Obsession:** Human dignity and behavior under extreme pressure; motion as meaning. Nature (rain, heat, wind, mud) as an active dramatic character — "I like extremes because I find them most alive."
- **Method:** From *Seven Samurai* on, shot with **multiple cameras rolling simultaneously + long telephoto lenses**, so actors never knew which camera was live — performances stayed natural, and he got coverage he could cut cleanly. Edited his own films. Painted every frame as storyboards on later films.
- **Signature:** Telephoto compression flattening chaotic battle into graphic planes; axial cuts; wipes as transitions; weather as emotion; dynamic screen-direction movement.
- **Films:** *Seven Samurai*, *Rashomon*, *Ikiru*, *Ran*, *Yojimbo*.
- **Steal:** Multiple cameras + long lenses to steal truthful, unselfconscious performance and cut it seamlessly.

### Yasujiro Ozu
- **Obsession:** The quiet dissolution of the family; *mono no aware* (the poignancy of transience). Life observed, not dramatized.
- **Method:** Radical subtraction and repetition. Locked the camera down — **nobody was allowed to touch it once placed.** Actors hit exact marks; he directed rhythm and gesture, not psychology.
- **Signature:** The **"tatami shot"** — camera ~3 feet off the ground, the eyeline of someone kneeling. Static frames characters enter and exit. Deliberate 360° space (breaking the 180° axis line on purpose). **"Pillow shots"** — cutaways to trains, alleys, empty rooms that hold time and mood.
- **Films:** *Tokyo Story*, *Late Spring*, *Early Summer*, *An Autumn Afternoon*.
- **Steal:** Stillness is a choice with force. A locked frame and an empty cutaway can carry more feeling than any camera move.

## II. The Metaphysicians (European Art Cinema)

### Ingmar Bergman
- **Obsession:** God's silence, death, the human face as the landscape of the soul. Marriage and psychic cruelty.
- **Method:** A repertory "family" of actors (Ullmann, von Sydow, Andersson) and crew (DP Sven Nykvist) he trusted absolutely — safety enabling extreme emotional exposure. Meticulous scripts, calm set, faith in the actor's face over spectacle.
- **Signature:** The **held close-up** — faces filling the frame, often two merging (*Persona*). Nykvist's soft, sourced, "invisible" light. Chamber scale; theatrical intensity.
- **Films:** *Persona*, *The Seventh Seal*, *Cries and Whispers*, *Wild Strawberries*, *Scenes from a Marriage*.
- **Steal:** The face is your widest canvas. Build a trusted troupe so actors will go where fear otherwise stops them.

### Andrei Tarkovsky
- **Obsession:** Time, memory, faith, the material world made spiritual. Cinema's unique gift is that it records *time itself.*
- **Method:** Defined directing literally as **"sculpting in time."** Rejected Eisenstein/montage: rhythm must come from **"time-pressure"** — the felt current of time *within* each shot — not from cutting. Long takes; water, fire, wind, weather in slow real duration.
- **Signature:** Extremely long, drifting takes; dreamlike shifts between color/monochrome; levitation, rain indoors, decaying interiors; zero exposition.
- **Films:** *Andrei Rublev*, *Mirror*, *Stalker*, *Solaris*, *Nostalghia*.
- **Steal:** The cut isn't the only rhythm. A shot has an internal tempo — learn to feel and hold it instead of always cutting away.

### Federico Fellini
- **Obsession:** Memory, dreams, spectacle, the circus of the self. The autobiographical made carnival.
- **Method:** Loose scripts; a ringmaster on set, casting for faces, dubbing dialogue later (Italian practice freed him to shout direction while cameras rolled). Chaos organized by a single sensibility.
- **Signature:** Grotesque and beautiful faces; parades and processions; the fluid boundary between fantasy and reality; Nino Rota's carnival scores. "Felliniesque."
- **Films:** *8½*, *La Dolce Vita*, *Amarcord*, *La Strada*, *Nights of Cabiria*.
- **Steal:** Mine your own memory and dreams without apology; let personal excess become style.

## III. New Hollywood and the American Auteurs

### Stanley Kubrick
- **Obsession:** Systems that crush the individual — war, tech, institutions; the cold gap between human intention and outcome. Perfection as moral seriousness.
- **Method:** Legendary obsessive control across every department; researched exhaustively. Notorious take counts — a rule of never printing before ~take 35; **127 takes** of one *Shining* scene. Pushed actors (Duvall) to genuine exhaustion to strip away performance. Owned lenses, edited meaning into architecture.
- **Signature:** One-point-perspective symmetry; slow reverse zooms; Steadicam glides (*The Shining* corridors); classical-music needle-drops as irony; clinical wide lenses.
- **Films:** *2001*, *A Clockwork Orange*, *The Shining*, *Dr. Strangelove*, *Barry Lyndon*, *Full Metal Jacket*.
- **Steal:** Repetition past the point of comfort can break an actor out of "acting." Control every variable so the accident that remains is the truth.

### Martin Scorsese
- **Obsession:** Guilt, sin, redemption, masculinity and violence; the Catholic conscience in a secular world. Cinema itself as devotion.
- **Method:** Meticulous shot-listing married to jazz-like energy; deep, decades-long collaboration with editor **Thelma Schoonmaker**. Rehearses through improvisation with actors (De Niro, DiCaprio), then locks it.
- **Signature:** Kinetic camera (whip pans, dolly, freeze frames), voiceover, needle-drop pop soundtracks, sudden violence, rapid associative cutting.
- **Films:** *Taxi Driver*, *Goodfellas*, *Raging Bull*, *The King of Comedy*, *The Irishman*.
- **Steal:** Music and cutting rhythm ARE the emotion. Edit to a beat; let a pop song do what a monologue can't.

### Steven Spielberg
- **Obsession:** Wonder, childhood, the fractured family, ordinary people meeting the extraordinary. The pure grammar of audience emotion.
- **Method:** Supreme visual clarity and blocking; stages long unbroken "oners" that hide their own complexity. Works fast, trusts instinct, protects the audience's emotional throughline. Lifelong scoring partnership with John Williams.
- **Signature:** The "Spielberg face" (awe in reaction shot before we see the object); pushing dolly-ins; light through fog/doorways; camera that moves *with* motivation, never showing off.
- **Films:** *Jaws*, *E.T.*, *Raiders of the Lost Ark*, *Jurassic Park*, *Schindler's List*, *Saving Private Ryan*.
- **Steal:** Show the *reaction* before the spectacle. Block the whole scene so the camera reveals story, not just records it.

### Francis Ford Coppola
- **Obsession:** Power, family, the American corruption of the soul; operatic scale and personal risk. The director as gambler who bets everything.
- **Method:** Theatrical rehearsal (actors' improvised "family dinners" to build bonds off-camera), then operatic staging. Willing to court chaos and near-ruin (*Apocalypse Now*) in pursuit of the true image.
- **Signature:** Chiaroscuro (Gordon Willis's "Prince of Darkness" lighting, eyes in shadow); cross-cutting ritual against violence (the baptism montage); slow tonal dread.
- **Films:** *The Godfather* I & II, *Apocalypse Now*, *The Conversation*.
- **Steal:** Build the ensemble's real relationships before you shoot; parallel editing can turn a plot event into a moral statement.

## IV. The Contemporary Stylists

### David Lynch
- **Obsession:** The rot beneath the American surface; the unconscious, dream logic, the ineffable. Intuition over explanation — meaning is *felt*, not decoded.
- **Method:** Refuses to explain his films even to actors; directs on gut and mood, treats sound design as half the movie. Transcendental Meditation as creative practice ("catching the big fish").
- **Signature:** Oppressive sound design/drones; red curtains, flickering light; non-linear dream structure; the uncanny in the mundane; unsettling slowness.
- **Films:** *Mulholland Drive*, *Blue Velvet*, *Eraserhead*, *Twin Peaks: Fire Walk with Me*, *Lost Highway*.
- **Steal:** Don't explain the mystery. Trust mood, sound, and image to reach the viewer below the level of plot.

### Coen Brothers (Joel & Ethan)
- **Obsession:** Cosmic irony, fate, human folly and greed; the indifferent universe. Control through writing.
- **Method:** Write together, storyboard fully, shoot exactly what they need — near-zero waste, minimal coverage. Long collaboration with DP Roger Deakins. Precise, deadpan control over tone.
- **Signature:** Genre pastiche with fatalism; symmetrical framing; regional dialects; sudden absurd violence; characters undone by their own schemes.
- **Films:** *No Country for Old Men*, *Fargo*, *The Big Lebowski*, *Miller's Crossing*, *A Serious Man*.
- **Steal:** Write it so tight you only shoot what cuts together. Tone control is authorship.

### Paul Thomas Anderson
- **Obsession:** Fathers and sons, damaged men, need and control, American striving as sickness. Ensemble as family.
- **Method:** Evolved from Scorsese-esque long takes and big ensembles to intimate, character-buried camera. Operates camera himself often; leaves room for actors (Day-Lewis, Hoffman) to breathe.
- **Signature:** Sinuous tracking shots and whip pans; long takes; anxious/dissonant scores (Jonny Greenwood); period texture; sudden emotional eruptions.
- **Films:** *There Will Be Blood*, *Magnolia*, *Boogie Nights*, *The Master*, *Phantom Thread*.
- **Steal:** Let the camera and score generate unease under a calm surface; give great actors long takes to live inside.

### David Fincher
- **Obsession:** Process, control, obsession itself; the procedural darkness of methodical people (killers, coders, cops). Precision as morality.
- **Method:** Famous for extreme take counts — the aim is to **exhaust the actor's self-consciousness until behavior becomes automatic and truthful** ("cinema is when you put an idea in somebody else's head"). Reframes "perfectionism" as diligence with tens of millions of dollars. Digital tools, invisible VFX, locked-off precision.
- **Signature:** Desaturated greens/ambers; smooth motion-controlled camera moves; impossible CG-assisted "camera" moves; obsessive symmetry and cleanliness.
- **Films:** *Se7en*, *Zodiac*, *The Social Network*, *Fight Club*, *Gone Girl*.
- **Steal:** Do enough takes that the acting falls away and only behavior is left. Then the camera holds still and lets it happen.

### Quentin Tarantino
- **Obsession:** Cinema as its own subject — genre, dialogue as music, delayed violence. The video-store cinephile as auteur.
- **Method:** Writes novelistic scripts; shoots on film by principle; theatrical, dialogue-forward; builds tension through talk, not action. Chapters and non-linear structure. Vows a finite filmography to protect the batting average.
- **Signature:** Trunk shots, foot shots, extended tension dialogue, sudden bursts of violence, needle-drops, non-linear chapters, homage.
- **Films:** *Pulp Fiction*, *Kill Bill*, *Inglourious Basterds*, *Reservoir Dogs*, *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*.
- **Steal:** Dialogue can BE the suspense. Stretch a conversation until the room is unbearable, then break it.

## V. The Modern Vanguard

### Christopher Nolan
- **Obsession:** Time, memory, subjectivity, obsession; the mind as a maze. Practical reality over digital fakery — the audience must *feel* it's real.
- **Method:** Shoots on **IMAX/large-format film**, in-camera practical effects (real 747 crash), avoids the video-village monitor. Structures scripts like puzzle-boxes; cross-cuts parallel timelines.
- **Signature:** Nested/fractured timelines; IMAX scale; ticking-clock cross-cutting; a low-end score pulse (Zimmer through *Dunkirk*, then Ludwig Göransson on *Tenet*/*Oppenheimer*); exposition-through-action. *(Full profile: `directors/christopher-nolan.md` — the source of truth for any director with a file in `directors/`.)*
- **Films:** *Inception*, *Memento*, *The Dark Knight*, *Interstellar*, *Dunkirk*, *Oppenheimer*.
- **Steal:** Structure IS content. Reorder time so the form of the film enacts its theme; shoot real things for real weight.

### Denis Villeneuve
- **Obsession:** Awe, dread, the human dwarfed by scale and silence. Image over word — **"Pure image and sound, that is the power of cinema... I don't remember movies because of a good line, I remember movies because of a strong image."** (Dialogue as "last resort.")
- **Method:** Storyboards atmosphere and scale; long silences; leans on DP (Deakins/Fraser), production design, and Zimmer's sound-mass. Slow builds, deliberate withholding.
- **Signature:** Monumental scale dwarfing figures; fog, monochrome deserts; oppressive low drones; minimal dialogue; patient, gliding camera.
- **Films:** *Dune* (I & II), *Blade Runner 2049*, *Arrival*, *Sicario*, *Prisoners*.
- **Steal:** Cut the dialogue you think you need. Let image, scale, and sound carry the scene — reach for a line only when nothing else works.

### Bong Joon-ho
- **Obsession:** Class, systems, the trapdoors under society; tonal whiplash (comedy → horror → tragedy in one scene). Genre as Trojan horse for politics.
- **Method:** Obsessive storyboarder — **boards every shot of every scene in advance** ("I'm always very nervous... if I don't prepare everything beforehand, I go crazy"); his boards stay ~99% intact in the final cut. Shoots only what he needs (little coverage). Controls the *frame and blocking* meticulously while giving actors freedom inside it.
- **Signature:** Vertical/architectural space as class metaphor (stairs, basements); precise blocking that traps characters; abrupt tonal shifts; symmetrical then destabilized framing.
- **Films:** *Parasite*, *Memories of Murder*, *Mother*, *The Host*, *Snowpiercer*.
- **Steal:** Pre-solve the movie on paper so the set has no panic. Use physical space (up/down) to say what dialogue can't.

### Wong Kar-wai
- **Obsession:** Longing, memory, missed connection, time slipping; mood over plot. Romantic melancholy as a total aesthetic.
- **Method:** The anti-Bong — **shoots with no finished script, no storyboards, no rehearsal**; discovers the film in the edit over months or years. Reshoots the same scene across locations until it "feels right" (*In the Mood for Love* took 15 months; cut up to the Cannes deadline). Trusts DP Christopher Doyle's camera and his own instinct.
- **Signature:** Step-printed slow-motion smearing motion; saturated color and neon; framing through doorways/mirrors/grilles; repeated music motifs; unresolved yearning.
- **Films:** *In the Mood for Love*, *Chungking Express*, *Happy Together*, *2046*, *Fallen Angels*.
- **Steal:** You can find the film in the process, not the script — if you're willing to shoot loose and sculpt endlessly in the edit. Mood can be the whole story.

### Greta Gerwig
- **Obsession:** Interiority of women and girls, coming-of-age, the tension between selfhood and the roles handed to women. Warmth married to formal rigor.
- **Method:** Deeply literary scripts; rehearsal and ensemble-building; creates a joyful, collaborative set. Non-linear structure used emotionally (*Little Women*'s intercut timelines). Actor-friendly, from her mumblecore-acting roots.
- **Signature:** Braided timelines contrasting past warmth/present grief; naturalistic overlapping dialogue; specific period and place; humor as emotional access.
- **Films:** *Lady Bird*, *Little Women*, *Barbie*.
- **Steal:** Restructure time to make theme land — put memory beside present so the audience feels the loss the character can't say.

### Jordan Peele
- **Obsession:** Social horror — racism, class, spectacle, complicity — encoded in genre. Horror as the most honest lens on American anxiety.
- **Method:** Comedy-honed sense of audience rhythm (setup/payoff) transposed to dread; meticulous symbolism and rewatch-reward layering; clear, classical suspense construction (openly Hitchcock/Spielberg-influenced).
- **Signature:** The unsettling held stare; symbolic imagery rewarding decoding; tonal control between laugh and scream; social subtext as literal plot.
- **Films:** *Get Out*, *Us*, *Nope*.
- **Steal:** Genre is a delivery system for ideas. Build a horror engine that also argues something true about the world.

### Alfonso Cuarón
- **Obsession:** Human resilience amid chaos and collapse; intimacy inside epic scale. Immersion — the viewer *inside* the moment.
- **Method:** Champion of the elaborate **long take** (with DP Emmanuel "Chivo" Lubezki) — choreographed oners that fuse actor, camera, and effects into unbroken real time. Shoots his own recent films as DP. Rigorous technical planning to enable apparent spontaneity.
- **Signature:** Extended tracking/handheld oners; deep-focus wide immersion; water and reflection; sound design placing you in space; unbroken duration = tension.
- **Films:** *Children of Men*, *Gravity*, *Roma*, *Y Tu Mamá También*.
- **Steal:** The unbroken take makes the audience a witness. Refuse the cut and time itself becomes suspense.

### Guillermo del Toro
- **Obsession:** Monsters as the moral heart; innocence vs. authoritarian cruelty; fairy tale as truth-telling. Love of the handmade creature.
- **Method:** Fills notebooks with detailed creature/design sketches; insists on practical effects and animatronics augmented by CG; builds richly colored, symbolic production design. Warm, prepared, generous on set.
- **Signature:** Amber/teal palette; clockwork and insect motifs; sympathetic monsters, human villains; storybook framing; vertical fairy-tale symbolism.
- **Films:** *Pan's Labyrinth*, *The Shape of Water*, *The Devil's Backbone*, *Crimson Peak*, *Pinocchio*.
- **Steal:** Design the whole world down to the eye of the creature; make the monster the one you love and the human the one you fear.

### Chloé Zhao
- **Obsession:** Belonging, grief, the individual against vast landscape; the dignity of ordinary and marginal lives. Blurring documentary and fiction.
- **Method:** Casts **non-professional actors playing versions of themselves** (real nomads, ranchers); loose scripts shaped around their real lives; small crews, natural light, magic-hour shooting. Terrence Malick-influenced lyricism.
- **Signature:** Golden-hour natural light; wide landscapes swallowing figures; documentary intimacy; drifting, observational rhythm; real faces.
- **Films:** *Nomadland*, *The Rider*, *Songs My Brothers Taught Me*.
- **Steal:** Cast the real people and let their lives write the scene. Nature's light, at the right hour, is your best production value.

## VI. Animation and the Craft Minds Behind the Camera

### Hayao Miyazaki
- **Obsession:** Flight, nature, pacifism, the moral complexity of children; anti-war and anti-industrial ecology. Wonder without villains.
- **Method:** Draws and corrects thousands of key frames by hand; famous for **"ma"** — deliberate pauses of stillness (a character just sitting, wind blowing) that let emotion settle. Storyboards ahead of a finished script, discovering the film as he draws.
- **Signature:** Hand-drawn detail; quiet non-action beats; morally gray "antagonists"; flight sequences; food and small domestic pleasures; strong young heroines.
- **Films:** *Spirited Away*, *My Neighbor Totoro*, *Princess Mononoke*, *Nausicaä*, *Howl's Moving Castle*.
- **Steal:** Build in stillness. The pause between events — "ma" — is where the audience actually feels.

### Roger Deakins (cinematographer)
- **Philosophy:** Cinematography **should never call attention to itself** — "create a world the audience is immersed in without one shot taking them out of it." Motivated, source-based light (lanterns in frame, household bulbs, bare fluorescents) for believable naturalism.
- **Signature:** Silhouettes against sky; single-source pools of light; restrained, precise camera; the *Blade Runner 2049* orange void, *1917*'s flare-lit night, *Skyfall*'s Shanghai glass.
- **Steal:** Light from where the light would actually come. Discipline and restraint read as beauty; showing off breaks the spell.

### Walter Murch (editor / sound designer)
- **Philosophy — the "Rule of Six":** rank a cut by six priorities, **Emotion (51%) > Story (23%) > Rhythm (10%) > Eye-trace (7%) > 2D plane (5%) > 3D spatial continuity (4%).** Sacrifice from the bottom up; **never give up emotion to preserve continuity** — "if the cut is emotionally right, the audience will accept any technical error." Coined "sound design"; edits standing up; the blink as a natural cut point.
- **Films:** *Apocalypse Now*, *The Conversation*, *The Godfather* trilogy, *The English Patient*.
- **Steal:** When two cuts fight, keep the one that's true to the feeling. Emotion outranks geography every time.

### Hans Zimmer (composer)
- **Approach:** Score as **texture, pulse, and sound design**, not just melody — build a sonic identity per film from unusual sources (the *Interstellar* church organ, the *Dune* processed vocals and invented instruments, the ticking-watch *Dunkirk* Shepard-tone). Embeds early in production; collaborates like a co-author (esp. with Nolan). Emotion via low-end physicality you feel in the chest.
- **Steal:** Sound can be built, not just written. Design a film's whole sonic world; let bass and drone do emotional work under the picture.

### Ennio Morricone (composer)
- **Approach:** The **theme as a character**; unconventional instrumentation and non-orchestral sounds (whistles, whip cracks, coyote howls, electric guitar, the human voice as lead) woven into the drama — often written *before* shooting so Leone could play it on set and cut to it. Opera-scaled emotion; the melody carries the film's soul.
- **Films (scores):** *The Good, the Bad and the Ugly*, *Once Upon a Time in the West*, *The Mission*, *Cinema Paradiso*.
- **Steal:** Give the film one unforgettable theme and let it recur as a character. Score can precede and shape the shoot, not just follow it.

## VII. Cross-Cutting Psychology of Great Directors

**1. Decisiveness under uncertainty.** A film set burns money by the minute; a director is a machine for making hundreds of irreversible choices fast with incomplete information. Spielberg, Kurosawa, and Hitchcock are prized for *speed of decision*. Even the "chaos" directors (Wong Kar-wai, Coppola) are decisive about *not deciding yet* — a deliberate strategy, not indecision.

**2. Taste as the real skill.** Technique is teachable; taste — knowing which take, which frame, which cut is *right* — is the scarce thing. Murch's Rule of Six is taste made explicit: a hierarchy of what to protect. Every great director is fundamentally an editor of options.

**3. Obsession and preparation as anxiety management.** Two opposite temperaments, same root. The **over-preparers** (Hitchcock, Kubrick, Bong, Fincher, Nolan) storyboard and pre-solve to eliminate surprise — Bong literally says planning stops him going "crazy." The **discoverers** (Wong Kar-wai, Malick, Fellini) court chaos to find something alive. Both are managing the same fear of the dead, obvious result; they just attack it from opposite ends.

**4. Handling fear and failure.** They treat failure as tuition. Coppola nearly destroyed himself on *Apocalypse Now* and called filmmaking a gamble you must be willing to lose. Kubrick's take counts are fear of the mediocre made ritual. Fincher reframes "perfectionism" as diligence — refusing shame about caring. The pattern: convert fear into *process* (more takes, more boards, more reshoots) rather than letting it freeze the decision.

**5. Protecting a singular vision.** The auteur's core job is saying *no* — to studios, notes, trends, and their own easy first idea. Kubrick controlled every department; Tarantino caps his filmography to protect the average; Villeneuve strips dialogue against every commercial instinct. Vision is defended by subtraction as much as insistence.

**6. Control vs. collaboration — a false binary.** The best run a paradox: **total control of the frame, real freedom inside it.** Bong controls blocking and camera absolutely but lets actors play; Kurosawa's multi-camera setup was *engineered* to free the actor; Fincher's endless takes exist to let behavior emerge past acting. And control is bought with long-term collaborators who become extensions of the vision — Scorsese/Schoonmaker, Bergman/Nykvist, Coens/Deakins, Nolan/Zimmer, Zhao/her real-life cast. Auteurism isn't solitude; it's a trusted crew pointed at one person's obsession.

**7. Cinema is manipulation of the viewer's mind, not the recording of events.** The through-line from Mamet (uninflected images juxtaposed to build meaning *in the audience's head*) to Hitchcock (control what the viewer knows) to Fincher ("put an idea in somebody else's head") to Villeneuve (image over word): the film does not happen on screen — it happens in the audience. Every technique above is ultimately a tool for engineering that internal experience.

## World cinema — the wider bench
Under-covered regions, one entry each (the front-of-house names have full files in `directors/`). Steal the method, not the nationality.

**Africa.** **Ousmane Sembène** (Senegal, "father of African cinema") — power abused at street level, told from the ordinary African's view in African languages; frontal patient wides, non-actors, a single object (a mask, a briefcase) carrying the political argument (*Black Girl*, *Xala*, *Moolaadé*). **Djibril Diop Mambéty** (Senegal) — the surreal opposite: jump-cut, collage sound decoupled from image, picaresque fable (*Touki Bouki*). **Souleymane Cissé** (Mali) — the supernatural shot in unbroken naturalistic light so the sacred reads as real (*Yeelen*). **Abderrahmane Sissako** (Mauritania/Mali) — tableau + off-screen sound; stages a huge political argument as literal theatre inside a domestic space, life flowing around it (*Timbuktu*, *Bamako*). **Mati Diop** (French-Senegalese) — genre (the ghost story) as the vehicle for migration's absence (*Atlantics*).

**Latin America.** **Glauber Rocha** (Brazil, Cinema Novo) — "aesthetic of hunger": turn budget-poverty into the film's identity; convulsive handheld, folk-Catholic iconography (*Black God, White Devil*). **Fernando Meirelles** (Brazil) — workshop-cast real neighborhood kids, then cut with music-video velocity so realism never drags (*City of God*). **Walter Salles** (Brazil) — the journey as spine; landscape does the emotional exposition dialogue withholds (*Central Station*, *The Motorcycle Diaries*). **Lucrecia Martel** (Argentina) — sound as the *primary* channel: dense off-screen audio and decentered, body-fragmenting framing carry the meaning the image hides (*The Headless Woman*, *Zama*). **Carlos Reygadas** (Mexico) — the real-time cosmic bookend: open/close on an unbroken natural event (a sunrise) until time itself is the subject (*Silent Light*). **Kleber Mendonça Filho** (Brazil) — architecture/property as the class battlefield, then a genre engine (siege, western) detonates the loaded tension (*Aquarius*, *Bacurau*).

**Middle East / Iran.** **Asghar Farhadi** (Iran) — the "everyone is right" construction: engineer conflict so both sides have a legitimate moral claim, then let withheld facts detonate the ambiguity (*A Separation*, *The Salesman*). **Jafar Panahi** (Iran) — turn the hardest constraint into the concept; made under a filmmaking ban, the limitation becomes the film's meaning (*This Is Not a Film*, *Taxi*). **Nadine Labaki** (Lebanon) — cast the actual people who lived the story, then shape it with unapologetic melodrama (*Capernaum*). **Elia Suleiman** (Palestine) — deadpan Tati-esque political comedy through locked symmetrical tableaux; one absurd action in the frame says what a monologue can't.

**East / Southeast Asia.** **Ozu Yasujirō** (Japan) — the tatami-level locked frame, near-eyeline frontal dialogue, and the **pillow shot** (a cutaway to an empty space to let emotion breathe); skips the melodramatic climax to show only its quiet aftermath (*Tokyo Story*). **Hou Hsiao-hsien** (Taiwan) — the distant long take that makes the viewer *find* the emotion; refuse the close-up, let duration and off-center staging carry it (*A City of Sadness*). **Edward Yang** (Taiwan) — glass, reflections, and architecture framing characters so the city's geometry externalizes their isolation (*Yi Yi*, *A Brighter Summer Day*). **Tsai Ming-liang** (Taiwan) — radical duration and silence; one recurring actor aging across the whole filmography (*Vive L'Amour*). **Apichatpong Weerasethakul** (Thailand) — the supernatural arrives unannounced inside a calm naturalistic take — no score sting, the ghost just sits down (*Uncle Boonmee*). **Jia Zhangke** (China) — shoot the real place *while it's vanishing* (Three Gorges mid-flood) so the film documents a disappearance that can't be restaged (*Still Life*). **Zhang Yimou** (China) — assign each act its own dominant color and design the whole frame around it, so color is structure, not decoration (*Raise the Red Lantern*, *Hero*).
