# Film Taxonomy — Genres, Types, Categories, Themes

A working map of genres, subgenres, tone, theme, and the categorization systems the industry uses. **Genre is a *promise* to the audience, not a shelf label** — it sets the emotional contract before the first frame. The "least" note under each genre is diagnostic: a genre fails when it delivers its surface conventions while breaking its emotional contract.

---

## 1. Primary Genres

Format: **Promise · Conventions · Contract · Structure · Top · Fails as.**

### Drama
- **Promise:** Emotional truth about how people actually live and change. **Conventions:** grounded stakes, interiority, moral ambiguity, dialogue-driven, realism over spectacle. **Contract:** "you'll feel something real and recognize yourself." **Structure:** character arc is the spine; a flawed protagonist forced to change or reveal it's impossible. **Top:** *The Godfather*, *Nomadland*, *There Will Be Blood*, *Manchester by the Sea*, *A Separation*. **Fails as:** melodrama with no earned emotion — misery for its own sake, issue-of-the-week moralizing, Big Feelings instead of behavior.

### Comedy
- **Promise:** pleasure and release through incongruity; the world is safe to laugh at. **Conventions:** setup/payoff, escalation, reversals, status games, rule-of-three, a comic engine. **Contract:** "you'll laugh and it won't cost you." **Structure:** looser than drama — a strong comic engine that generates scenes; still needs a real want underneath. **Top:** *Some Like It Hot*, *Groundhog Day*, *Dr. Strangelove*, *Airplane!*, *Superbad*. **Fails as:** joke density with no character or stakes — reference-spam, improv that never lands a scene.

### Action
- **Promise:** kinetic excitement; competence and danger made visible. **Conventions:** set pieces, escalating physical stakes, a clear antagonist, chases/fights/stunts, ticking clocks, spatial clarity. **Contract:** "you'll be thrilled and the good guys will (usually) win." **Structure:** escalating action beats on a simple legible want; climax is the biggest confrontation. **Top:** *Die Hard*, *Mad Max: Fury Road*, *Terminator 2*, *John Wick*, *Raiders of the Lost Ark*. **Fails as:** incoherent geography and weightless CGI — shaky-cam mush, stakes with no emotional anchor.

### Thriller
- **Promise:** sustained dread; something is about to go wrong. **Conventions:** suspense over surprise (the audience knows the bomb is there), ticking clocks, a competent antagonist, paranoia, reversals, ordinary-person-in-over-their-head. **Contract:** "you'll be tense and the tension will pay off." **Structure:** escalating jeopardy; protagonist loses control then claws it back; built on dramatic irony. **Top:** *Rear Window*, *Seven*, *No Country for Old Men*, *Zodiac*, *Prisoners*. **Fails as:** contrived suspense — idiot-plot decisions, cheating red herrings, a resolution that dishonors the setup.

### Horror
- **Promise:** safe encounter with mortality, the monstrous, the taboo — catharsis through fear. **Conventions:** dread-building, the threat (seen or unseen), violation of body or safe space, escalating rules, the confrontation; sound design is load-bearing. **Contract:** "you'll be scared and survive it." **Structure:** normalcy → intrusion → escalation → siege → confrontation (or bleak inversion). **Top:** *The Exorcist*, *Alien*, *The Shining*, *Hereditary*, *Get Out*. **Fails as:** jump-scare vending machine — no dread, characters as body-count, a threat with no rules or meaning.

### Science Fiction
- **Promise:** "what if?" — extrapolation from science/tech into consequence. **Conventions:** a speculative premise with internal rigor, worldbuilding, the novum (the one new thing) that reorganizes society, ideas as engine. **Contract:** "you'll see an idea followed honestly to its end." **Structure:** premise established → logic explored → human cost paid. **Top:** *2001*, *Blade Runner*, *Arrival*, *Children of Men*, *The Matrix*. **Fails as:** set-dressing sci-fi — lasers wrapped around a generic plot, self-contradicting worldbuilding, ideas name-dropped not dramatized.

### Fantasy
- **Promise:** wonder; a coherent world where the impossible obeys rules. **Conventions:** consistent magic system, quest, chosen/reluctant hero, mythic stakes, secondary-world logic. **Contract:** "you'll believe a world that can't exist because it obeys its own laws." **Structure:** often hero's journey / quest; a threat to the world's order, a fellowship, a reckoning. **Top:** *The Lord of the Rings*, *Pan's Labyrinth*, *Spirited Away*, *The Princess Bride*, *Willow*. **Fails as:** rule-less magic — deus ex machina powers, worldbuilding as info-dump, stakes so cosmic they stop meaning anything.

### Romance
- **Promise:** the ache and reward of intimacy; connection is possible. **Conventions:** the meet, the obstacle (internal or external), yearning, the near-loss, the choice; chemistry is the whole ballgame. **Contract:** "two people who should be together, kept apart by something real, then united — or heartbreakingly not." **Structure:** attraction → obstacle → escalation → false loss → resolution; the obstacle must be credible. **Top:** *Casablanca*, *Brief Encounter*, *Before Sunrise*, *In the Mood for Love*, *Portrait of a Lady on Fire*. **Fails as:** manufactured obstacle — a conflict one honest conversation would end, chemistry-free leads, love as reward for no growth.

### Crime
- **Promise:** the machinery of transgression and consequence. **Conventions:** the job/the code, cops-and-criminals, procedure, the moral cost, betrayal, the fall. **Contract:** "you'll be pulled into a world with its own rules and watch it exact its price." **Structure:** rise-and-fall (gangster), the job and its unraveling (heist), or the case and its solve (procedural). **Top:** *Goodfellas*, *Heat*, *Chinatown*, *City of God*, *The Departed*. **Fails as:** glamour without cost — crime as cool, procedure with no character, cardboard cops and criminals.

### Mystery / Noir
- **Promise:** the pleasure of concealment and revelation. **Conventions (mystery):** a crime/puzzle, clues fairly planted, a detective, red herrings, the recontextualizing reveal. **Noir adds:** moral fog, fatalism, the femme fatale, chiaroscuro, a doomed/compromised protagonist, voiceover. **Contract:** "the truth is knowable and getting there is the reward" — noir adds "but it may destroy you." **Structure:** investigation as engine; each clue narrows the field until the reversal; noir bends toward corruption and defeat. **Top:** *The Third Man*, *Double Indemnity*, *Chinatown*, *Knives Out*, *Memento*. **Fails as:** unfair mystery (solution pulled from nowhere); noir-as-costume (the trench coat, none of the fatalism).

### War
- **Promise:** the extremity of human experience under organized violence. **Conventions:** the unit, the mission, the cost, the moral rupture, chaos made legible, brotherhood, indifference. **Contract:** "you'll witness the truth of combat and be changed" (or anti-war: "you'll see the lie"). **Structure:** mission/campaign as spine; often ensemble, often a descent from ideals into reality. **Top:** *Apocalypse Now*, *Come and See*, *Saving Private Ryan*, *Paths of Glory*, *All Quiet on the Western Front*. **Fails as:** recruitment-poster spectacle — war as thrill ride, sacrifice as sentiment, enemy as faceless target.

### Western
- **Promise:** the myth of the frontier — order vs. wilderness, individual vs. civilization. **Conventions:** the lone gunman, the town, the showdown, the code, the landscape as character, the closing frontier. **Contract:** "you'll watch a man face violence and the end of an era." **Structure:** a stranger arrives / a wrong demands answering; escalation to the duel; revisionist westerns invert the myth. **Top:** *The Searchers*, *Once Upon a Time in the West*, *Unforgiven*, *The Good, the Bad and the Ugly*, *No Country for Old Men* (neo). **Fails as:** nostalgia without interrogation — white-hat/black-hat cliché, landscape as postcard.

### Musical
- **Promise:** emotion overflowing into song and movement. **Conventions:** the "I want" song, numbers that advance story or reveal interiority, integration of song/dance/plot, heightened reality. **Contract:** "when feeling gets too big for words it becomes music — and you'll go with it." **Structure:** numbers as emotional beats mapped to the arc; the placement of songs *is* the dramaturgy. **Top:** *Singin' in the Rain*, *West Side Story*, *Cabaret*, *La La Land*, *The Umbrellas of Cherbourg*. **Fails as:** songs that stop the story — numbers as interruption not escalation, jukebox pastiche with no reason to sing.

### Adventure
- **Promise:** the thrill of the journey into the unknown; discovery. **Conventions:** a quest/expedition, exotic locales, obstacles-as-episodes, a goal, the map. **Contract:** "you'll be swept somewhere far away and come back changed." **Structure:** episodic pursuit toward a goal; each locale a new test; often married to action or fantasy. **Top:** *Raiders of the Lost Ark*, *The Treasure of the Sierra Madre*, *Lawrence of Arabia*, *The Goonies*, *Jurassic Park*. **Fails as:** travelogue with no jeopardy — episodes that don't build, a goal nobody's invested in.

### Historical / Period
- **Promise:** the past made present and consequential. **Conventions:** period-accurate design, real events/eras, the tension between then and now, scale. **Contract:** "you'll live inside a time not your own and understand it from within." **Structure:** drama or epic scaffolding; the period is the pressure, not the point. **Top:** *Barry Lyndon*, *The Favourite*, *12 Years a Slave*, *Amadeus*, *Ran*. **Fails as:** costume-drama waxworks — reverent, inert, history homework; production design smothering story.

### Biopic
- **Promise:** the truth of a real life; how a person became who they were. **Conventions:** a defining struggle, the ascent montage, the fall and recovery, the great moments, the reckoning with legacy. **Contract:** "you'll understand a real human being, not their Wikipedia entry." **Structure:** cradle-to-grave or a single decisive window; the best pick a lens rather than cover everything. **Top:** *Raging Bull*, *Lawrence of Arabia*, *Amadeus*, *The Social Network*, *I'm Not There*. **Fails as:** greatest-hits hagiography — checklist of famous events, no interiority, sanded-down subject.

### Documentary
- **Promise:** the real, framed to reveal truth. **Conventions:** actuality footage, interviews, archival, observation, narration or its refusal; a thesis or question (modes in §5). **Contract:** "this happened, and it means something." **Structure:** argument (essayistic), chronicle (observational), or investigation (the reveal); story shaped from the real. **Top:** *Shoah*, *Hoop Dreams*, *Man on Wire*, *The Act of Killing*, *Grizzly Man*. **Fails as:** talking-heads sludge, or covert advocacy — footage with no argument, or a thesis that ignores counter-evidence.

### Animation *(a medium, not a genre — hosts every genre)*
- **Promise:** anything imaginable, made to move. **Conventions:** stylization, physicality (squash-and-stretch), impossible worlds, expressive design. **Contract:** genre-dependent, but always "you'll see something that could exist no other way." **Top:** *Spirited Away*, *Toy Story*, *Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse*, *Grave of the Fireflies*, *Persepolis*. **Fails as:** animation as babysitting — reference filler, celebrity-voice cynicism, motion with no design intent.

### Family
- **Promise:** a story that works for a child and an adult at once; wonder without cruelty. **Conventions:** a young protagonist or child's-eye view, clear morality, dual-register humor, hope. **Contract:** "everyone in the room will be moved and no one harmed by it." **Structure:** simple legible arc; a lesson earned through adventure or relationship. **Top:** *E.T.*, *Paddington 2*, *The Iron Giant*, *My Neighbor Totoro*, *Babe*. **Fails as:** cynical four-quadrant product — pandering, fart-joke filler, sentiment substituted for feeling.

### Sports
- **Promise:** the underdog's shot; effort rewarded (or nobly not). **Conventions:** the training montage, the mentor, the rival, the big game, the team-as-family, the setback before the final push. **Contract:** "you'll invest in someone's striving and feel the outcome in your body." **Structure:** highly reliable — call to compete → training → setback → final contest; the sport dramatizes an inner want. **Top:** *Raging Bull*, *Rocky*, *Hoop Dreams* (doc), *Moneyball*, *Warrior*. **Fails as:** formula on autopilot — clichés unearned, the game with no stakes beyond winning.

### Disaster
- **Promise:** survival against overwhelming force; humanity vs. the uncontrollable. **Conventions:** the ensemble cross-section, the warning ignored, the event, the escape, sacrifice, the expert nobody listened to. **Contract:** "you'll watch ordinary people face annihilation and root for who lives." **Structure:** calm → warning → catastrophe → survival gauntlet → aftermath. **Top:** *The Poseidon Adventure*, *Titanic*, *Deep Impact*, *The Impossible*, *Contagion*. **Fails as:** effects reel with cardboard people — characters as fodder, sentiment glued on to justify the destruction.

### Superhero
- **Promise:** power and responsibility; the fantasy of significance with a moral test. **Conventions:** origin, powers-and-limits, the villain as thematic mirror, the secret identity, the cost of power, the city to save. **Contract:** "you'll watch someone become more than human and choose what to do with it." **Structure:** origin (become) or established-hero (test); the villain externalizes the hero's flaw. **Top:** *The Dark Knight*, *Spider-Man 2*, *Logan*, *Into the Spider-Verse*, *The Incredibles*. **Fails as:** interchangeable CGI sky-beam — quippy weightlessness, stakes reset next installment, a villain who's a plot device not a mirror.

## 2. Subgenres & Hybrids (the one defining thing)

- **Rom-com:** comedy engine driving a romance obstacle — the humor *is* the courtship.
- **Dark comedy:** laughter aimed at what we're not supposed to laugh at (death, cruelty, despair).
- **Slasher:** a (masked, often human) killer picks off a group one by one; the kill is the set piece.
- **Psychological horror:** the threat is the mind — dread from instability, not a monster you can point at.
- **Body horror:** the violation is of the body itself — transformation, infection, grotesque mutation.
- **Folk horror:** terror rooted in landscape, old belief, and an isolated community's relationship to the land.
- **Space opera:** melodrama and mythic conflict at galactic scale — romance and empire, science optional.
- **Cyberpunk:** high tech, low life — body/mind commodified under corporate dystopia.
- **Dystopia:** a society organized around a controlling idea taken to its oppressive conclusion.
- **Time-travel:** causality itself becomes the antagonist and the puzzle.
- **Heist:** the plan and its execution — pleasure from competence, the crew, and what goes wrong.
- **Neo-noir:** noir's fatalism and moral fog updated past the classic era.
- **Courtroom drama:** truth adjudicated through procedure — the trial is the battlefield.
- **Coming-of-age:** a threshold crossing from innocence to experience; the protagonist ends someone new.
- **Road movie:** physical journey as engine of internal change; the map is the arc.
- **Revenge:** a wrong demands balancing, and the pursuit consumes the pursuer.
- **Gangster / mob:** the rise and fall inside a criminal hierarchy; loyalty and its betrayal.
- **Spy:** deception as profession — identity, tradecraft, and who you can trust.
- **Martial arts:** the body as instrument; choreography as narrative and philosophy.
- **Monster:** a creature that embodies a fear; the film is our negotiation with it.
- **Found footage:** the camera exists *in* the story — recorded, discovered, "real."
- **Mockumentary:** documentary form applied to fiction, usually satire — reality's aesthetic played straight.
- **Satire:** ridicule with a target — mockery in service of a critique.
- **Tragicomedy:** comedy and tragedy in the same hand, neither resolving the other.
- **Magical realism:** the impossible treated as unremarkable within an otherwise real world.
- **Epic:** vast scale — of time, geography, or stakes — an individual carried by history.
- **Techno-thriller:** suspense driven by technology/systems and their failure or weaponization.
- **Political thriller:** suspense inside the machinery of power — conspiracy, statecraft, narrative control.
- **Erotic thriller:** desire and danger fused — sex is the trap and the tell.
- **Conspiracy thriller:** the paranoid protagonist discovers a hidden system bigger than they are.

## 3. Tone & Mood (a craft axis, independent of genre)

Tone is chosen and controlled through image, sound, pacing, performance, and word — two war films can be elegiac or gung-ho. Tonal consistency (or deliberate, controlled rupture — see Bong Joon-ho) is a mark of directorial command. Set by the *interaction* of elements: the same comedic script in gritty handheld under a desaturated grade reads nothing like it does in warm, symmetrical, scored whimsy.

Comedic · Dramatic · Dark · Whimsical · Gritty · Satirical · Melancholic · Absurdist · Elegiac · Uplifting · Bleak.

## 4. Themes / Thematic Archetypes

Theme = the argument the film makes about being human. Format: **dramatic question → a film that nails it.**

- **Coming of age** — *who am I becoming as I lose my innocence?* → *Boyhood*.
- **Redemption** — *can a person be more than their worst act?* → *The Shawshank Redemption*.
- **Revenge** — *does avenging a wrong restore us, or destroy us?* → *Oldboy*.
- **Love vs. duty** — *what do we owe others versus ourselves?* → *Casablanca*.
- **Man vs. nature** — *can the individual survive an indifferent world?* → *The Revenant*.
- **Man vs. society** — *can one person stand against the collective?* → *One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest*.
- **Man vs. self** — *can we overcome our own nature?* → *Black Swan*.
- **Sacrifice** — *what is worth giving up everything for?* → *Sophie's Choice*.
- **Corruption of power** — *does power reveal us or rot us?* → *There Will Be Blood*.
- **Identity** — *who are we when the masks come off?* → *Persona*.
- **Isolation** — *can we endure being truly alone?* → *Cast Away*.
- **Class** — *is the ladder real, and what does climbing it cost?* → *Parasite*.
- **Family** — *what binds us to the people we didn't choose?* → *The Godfather*.
- **Mortality** — *how do we live knowing we die?* → *Ikiru*.
- **Freedom** — *what is liberty worth, and to whom?* → *12 Years a Slave*.
- **Good vs. evil** — *where is the line, and can we hold it?* → *The Lord of the Rings*.
- **Loss of innocence** — *what is gone once we truly see the world?* → *Pan's Labyrinth*.
- **Alienation** — *why can't we connect in a crowded world?* → *Taxi Driver*.
- **Justice** — *what does a fair reckoning actually require?* → *12 Angry Men*.
- **Obsession** — *when does devotion become self-destruction?* → *Whiplash*.
- **Fate vs. free will** — *do we choose, or are we chosen?* → *No Country for Old Men*.
- **The outsider** — *can one who doesn't belong ever be let in?* → *Edward Scissorhands*.

## 5. Categorization Systems the Industry Uses

Professionals sort a film along several axes at once. (*Parasite* = drama/thriller · dark-satirical tone · class theme · mid-budget · narrative · Korean New Wave lineage.)

**By genre / subgenre** (§1–2) — marketing and development shorthand; drives audience expectation, festival slotting, streaming category.

**By format / length:** feature (≈75+ min; theatrical ~90–120) · short (<40 min) · featurette/mid-length (~40–75) · series/episodic (drama hour, half-hour, limited/anthology) · micro-forms (music video, commercial, social/vertical, webisode).

**By budget tier** (drives crew, schedule, risk): micro (<~$250k) · low/indie (~$250k–$5M) · mid-budget studio (~$5M–$60M) · tentpole/blockbuster (~$100M+). The vanishing "mid-budget adult drama" is itself an industry talking point.

**By audience / rating:** MPA (US): G, PG, PG-13, R, NC-17 · BBFC (UK): U, PG, 12A/12, 15, 18 · India CBFC: U, UA, A, S · "four-quadrant" (all ages/genders) vs. targeted · TV: TV-Y through TV-MA.

**By mode (relation to reality):** narrative/fiction · documentary (modes: expository, observational/direct cinema, participatory, reflexive, poetic, performative) · experimental/avant-garde · animation (a production mode crossing all) · hybrid (docudrama, docufiction, mockumentary).

**By era / movement:**
- **German Expressionism** (~1919–31) — distorted subjective sets and shadow (*Caligari*, *Metropolis*).
- **Soviet Montage** (1920s) — meaning manufactured through editing/collision (*Battleship Potemkin*).
- **Italian Neorealism** (~1942–51) — location shooting, non-professional actors, postwar working-class life (*Bicycle Thieves*).
- **French New Wave** (~1959–64) — auteur theory, jump cuts, on-location spontaneity, self-aware form (*Breathless*, *The 400 Blows*).
- **New Hollywood** (~1967–80) — director-driven, morally ambiguous, European-influenced (*Bonnie and Clyde*, *The Godfather*, *Taxi Driver*).
- **Dogme 95** (1995) — the ascetic "Vow of Chastity": location only, no added light/sound/props, handheld (*The Celebration*).
- **Also worth knowing:** Film Noir (1940s–50s), Japanese Golden Age (1950s), British kitchen-sink (late 50s–60s), New German Cinema (1970s), Hong Kong New Wave, Iranian New Wave, Indian Parallel Cinema (Ray/Ghatak/Sen), mumblecore (2000s), Romanian New Wave (2000s), Korean New Wave (2000s–).

## Notes on use
- **Genre is a contract, not a cage.** The strongest films honor the promise while deliberately subverting one convention (Bong, the Coens, Peele). Subversion only works if the base contract is understood first.
- **Animation and documentary are modes, not genres** — they host every genre; categorize on multiple axes at once.
