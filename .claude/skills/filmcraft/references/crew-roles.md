# The Professional Filmmaking Crew: Complete Role Reference

Convention: **OWN** = the one decision/deliverable that is theirs alone. **GOOD** = what mastery looks like. **FAIL** = common failure modes. **LINE** = reports to / hands off to.

Above-the-Line (ATL) = creative/deal principals (writer, director, producers, lead cast). Below-the-Line (BTL) = the crafts executing the plan. Format notes flag where features, TV/streaming, docs, commercials, and music videos diverge.

---

## 1. Development & Producing

### Screenwriter
- **OWN:** The blueprint — structure, scenes, dialogue. The words on the page everyone else interprets.
- **GOOD:** Story engine that survives production; dialogue that reveals character through action; a shootable script (scene economy, clear geography) not just a readable one.
- **FAIL:** Overwriting (unshootable stage directions, budget-blind set pieces); on-the-nose dialogue; structural sag in act two; precious about cuts.
- **LINE:** In features, sells/reports to producer; often replaced or joined by other writers. In TV, subordinate to the showrunner.

### Showrunner (TV/streaming)
- **OWN:** The show's creative and operational bible — tone, arc, and the final creative call across every episode and department, plus the writers' room and budget.
- **GOOD:** Consistent voice across 8–10 episodes and multiple directors; a functioning room; delivering on schedule/budget without diluting vision. The rare hybrid of head writer and top executive.
- **FAIL:** Bottlenecking (every decision waits on them); room dysfunction; scripts late, pushing production into chaos; can't separate writing talent from management.
- **LINE:** Reports to the network/studio and EPs; every writer, director, and department head ultimately answers to them. The TV equivalent of the film director's authority.

### Executive Producer (EP)
- **OWN:** The money and the mandate — securing financing and/or wielding the top-level authority that greenlights the project. Ties the deal together.
- **GOOD:** Real financing or packaging clout; protects the project's interests without micromanaging craft; opens doors (talent, distribution).
- **FAIL:** Vanity/credit-only EPs with no function; meddling in creative they don't understand; money that arrives late or evaporates.
- **LINE:** Top of the producing chain; the below-them producers and line producer report up. In TV the title often means senior writer/showrunner, a different animal from the film financier EP.

### Producer
- **OWN:** The project itself, end to end — develops it, hires the director and key crew, and carries it from concept through delivery. The buck stops here creatively-operationally.
- **GOOD:** Great taste in material and people; keeps creative ambition and financial reality in the same room; problem-solver who protects the director while protecting the money.
- **FAIL:** Loses control of budget or schedule; hires a director then fights them; can't say no; drama between financiers and the floor.
- **LINE:** Reports to EPs/financiers/studio; hires and oversees line producer, director, department heads.

### Line Producer
- **OWN:** The budget and schedule as an executable plan — every below-the-line dollar and day. Turns the script into a costed, scheduled shoot.
- **GOOD:** Accurate budgets that hold; realistic boards; deals that stretch money; knows every department's real cost. The financial conscience of the floor.
- **FAIL:** Optimistic budgets that blow up; underscheduling that forces cut scenes; penny-wise choices that cost more later; adversarial with departments.
- **LINE:** Reports to producer/EP; oversees UPM, production coordinator, and all BTL department budgets. Usually not involved in raising the money — that's the EP.

### Unit Production Manager (UPM)
- **OWN:** Day-to-day logistics execution — crew hiring, contracts, permits, equipment, the physical machinery of the shoot running on time and on budget.
- **GOOD:** Nothing surprises them; paperwork/union compliance airtight; departments get what they need before they ask; cost reports current.
- **FAIL:** Missing permits/insurance; crew or gear no-shows; overtime blowouts; compliance failures that shut a set down.
- **LINE:** Reports to line producer/producer; works hand-in-glove with the 1st AD (UPM owns the money/logistics, 1st AD owns the shooting day). Guild role (DGA in the US).

### Associate Producer / Co-Producer
- **OWN:** A delegated slice of producing — a specific essential function (post, a location, a financing tranche, significant development contribution). Definition flexes per production.
- **GOOD:** Genuinely carries a real load; the credit reflects work, not favor.
- **FAIL:** Empty credit given as a favor or perk; unclear scope causing overlap.
- **LINE:** Reports to the producer. Co-producer generally ranks above associate producer.

### Development Executive
- **OWN:** The slate pipeline — finding, evaluating, and shepherding material (scripts, IP, pitches) toward greenlight. Coverage in, greenlights out.
- **GOOD:** Sharp taste; gives notes that improve scripts without homogenizing them; manages writer relationships; spots commercial + creative fit.
- **FAIL:** Development hell (endless notes, no decisions); generic "make it more relatable" notes; kills good projects, champions bad ones.
- **LINE:** Studio/production-company side; reports to head of development/production; hands greenlit projects to producers.

## 2. Direction

### Director
- **OWN:** The unified vision — every creative choice (performance, camera, tone, cut) serves one coherent film. The final creative interpreter of the script on features.
- **GOOD:** Clear vision communicated so every department pulls the same direction; gets performances; makes decisive choices under time pressure; knows what to fight for and what to let go.
- **FAIL:** Indecision (burns the day); no shot plan; can't talk to actors; changes vision mid-shoot; over-shoots coverage they'll never use; loses the day chasing perfection.
- **LINE:** In features, answers to the producer but holds creative authority on the floor. In TV, subordinate to the showrunner (episodic directors are visiting craftspeople). In commercials, serves the agency/client brief. Hands the cut to the editor.

### First Assistant Director (1st AD)
- **OWN:** The shooting day — the schedule, the call sheet, the set's tempo and safety. Not a creative deputy; the director's operational engine.
- **GOOD:** Runs a set that makes the day without feeling rushed; anticipates the next setup; owns safety; the director directs while the 1st AD drives everything else.
- **FAIL:** Loses the day; chaotic set; safety lapses; can't manage the director's pace; bad call sheets; poor communication between departments.
- **LINE:** Reports to director and UPM/producer; directs the 2nd AD, 2nd 2nd, and PAs; commands the floor. DGA role.

### Second Assistant Director (2nd AD)
- **OWN:** The paperwork and the people flow — call sheets, production reports, and getting cast through hair/makeup/wardrobe to set on time.
- **GOOD:** Accurate call sheets out on time; talent always ready when needed; background wrangled; the 1st AD never has to chase status.
- **FAIL:** Late/wrong call sheets; actors not ready; background chaos; missing paperwork.
- **LINE:** Reports to the 1st AD; supervises 2nd 2nd AD, set PAs, and background.

### Script Supervisor ("Scripty")
- **OWN:** Continuity and coverage — the authoritative record that the footage will cut together. Tracks matching (props, wardrobe, action, eyelines, screen direction) and logs every take.
- **GOOD:** Catches the mismatch before it's shot; guarantees editorial has the coverage and matching to cut; lined script and notes an editor can trust; watches the 180-degree line.
- **FAIL:** Missed continuity error that costs a reshoot or breaks a cut; incomplete notes; misses a hole in coverage; doesn't flag an unusable eyeline.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director; the critical liaison to editorial (their notes drive the edit). Independent of the AD department.

### Second-Unit Director
- **OWN:** Everything the main unit doesn't shoot — inserts, establishing shots, action/stunts, plates — matched seamlessly to the main unit's look.
- **GOOD:** Footage that cuts invisibly into the first unit; relieves the main-unit schedule; delivers action/scale the director can't cover in the main day.
- **FAIL:** Tone/look mismatch; footage that screams "second unit"; scope creep that duplicates main-unit work.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director/producer; runs a parallel unit with its own DP and crew.

## 3. Camera Department

### Director of Photography / Cinematographer (DP/DoP)
- **OWN:** The image — lighting, lens, framing, exposure, and camera movement that render the director's vision. Owns the look and commands both camera and grip/electric.
- **GOOD:** A visual language that serves story, not just "pretty"; consistent look across days/locations; efficient lighting that keeps pace; a strong creative partnership with the director.
- **FAIL:** Beautiful but story-blind images; slow lighting that kills the schedule; inconsistent look; fighting the director's vision; can't work to budget.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director; heads the camera operator, ACs, DIT, gaffer, and key grip. ATL-adjacent (ASC/BSC etc.). In small docs/commercials, often self-operates.

### Camera Operator
- **OWN:** The frame in motion — executing composition and movement live, take by take, within the DP's lighting and the director's blocking.
- **GOOD:** Instinctive framing that finds the moment; smooth, motivated movement; keeps the shot even when actors go off-mark; flags a soft or missed take.
- **FAIL:** Sloppy framing; missed the moment; jerky moves; doesn't call a bad take.
- **LINE:** Reports to the DP. On smaller productions the DP operates and this role vanishes.

### First Assistant Camera / Focus Puller (1st AC)
- **OWN:** Focus — keeping the intended subject sharp on a moving target, blind, in real time. Also owns camera prep, build, and maintenance.
- **GOOD:** Tack-sharp focus at wide apertures on unpredictable movement; a camera package that never fails; marks and measures obsessively.
- **FAIL:** Soft focus (the single most common cause of an otherwise-perfect take being unusable); camera malfunction from bad prep; missed marks.
- **LINE:** Reports to the camera operator/DP; supervises the 2nd AC.

### Second Assistant Camera (2nd AC)
- **OWN:** The slate and the media chain — marking takes, camera reports, lens/filter changes, and shepherding media to the DIT. Owns the paper/data trail of the image.
- **GOOD:** Clean slates, accurate reports, no media confusion, magazines/cards always ready; the 1st AC never waits.
- **FAIL:** Mislabeled media or reports (sync/editorial nightmares); slate errors; lost/overwritten cards.
- **LINE:** Reports to the 1st AC; hands media to the DIT/loader.

### Digital Imaging Technician (DIT)
- **OWN:** Image data integrity and on-set look management — safe backups (checksummed, redundant), exposure/color monitoring, and the on-set color pipeline to post.
- **GOOD:** Zero data loss, verified backups; a look/LUT that carries cleanly into the grade; catches exposure/clipping the eye misses on set.
- **FAIL:** Data loss (catastrophic, unrecoverable footage); unmanaged looks that fight the colorist; a pipeline post can't use.
- **LINE:** Reports to the DP; bridges camera and post. The digital-era successor to the film loader.

### Gaffer (Chief Lighting Technician)
- **OWN:** Realizing the DP's lighting — the lighting plan, fixtures, power, and the electric crew that rigs it. Owns light and power on set.
- **GOOD:** Translates a DP's intent into a fast, safe rig; anticipates the next setup's needs; solves lighting problems creatively and quickly; power distribution that never trips.
- **FAIL:** Slow rigging; unsafe electrical; can't interpret the DP; blows the schedule with over-elaborate setups.
- **LINE:** Reports to the DP; heads the electric department, directs the best boy electric.

### Best Boy Electric
- **OWN:** The electric department's logistics — crew scheduling, gear orders, truck/inventory, timecards. The gaffer's second-in-command running the department's machinery.
- **GOOD:** Right gear on the truck, right crew on the day; nothing missing when the gaffer calls for it; budget-aware ordering.
- **FAIL:** Missing/wrong equipment; understaffed; disorganized truck; rental overages.
- **LINE:** Reports to the gaffer; manages the electricians.

### Key Grip
- **OWN:** Grip and rigging — camera support (dolly, crane), and all non-electric light shaping (flags, nets, silks, overheads). Owns everything that holds or shapes, in partnership with the gaffer.
- **GOOD:** Safe, fast camera movement and rigging; shapes light hand-in-glove with the gaffer; solves mounting problems for any surface or vehicle; safety-first.
- **FAIL:** Unsafe rigs (a lead risk to life); slow dolly/crane setups; poor light control; can't execute a move.
- **LINE:** Reports to the DP; heads the grip department, directs the best boy grip and dolly grip. (Gaffer = electric/light; key grip = rigging/light-shaping — they split the DP's physical needs.)

### Grips / Best Boy Grip / Dolly Grip
- **OWN:** Best boy grip runs grip logistics/crew (mirror of best boy electric). Dolly grip owns the physical dolly/crane move — laying track, executing the push/pull in sync with focus and action.
- **GOOD:** A dolly move so smooth it's invisible and perfectly timed to the actor and the focus pull.
- **FAIL:** Bumpy or mistimed moves; bad track; unsafe rigging.
- **LINE:** Report to the key grip.

## 4. Art Department

### Production Designer (PD)
- **OWN:** The total visual world — sets, locations, color, texture, period, the physical look of everything on camera except wardrobe/hair/makeup. The design counterpart to the DP.
- **GOOD:** A cohesive world that tells story silently; design that reads on camera and to budget; close collaboration with the DP so sets light well.
- **FAIL:** Design that fights the story or the budget; sets that don't light; inconsistent world; over-designing what's never seen.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director; heads art director, set decorator, props, construction. ATL-adjacent.

### Art Director
- **OWN:** Executing the PD's design — running the art department, budgets, construction schedules, and crews to build what the PD drew.
- **GOOD:** Sets built on time and budget to spec; manages construction/scenic/props crews; problem-solves the buildable version of the PD's vision.
- **FAIL:** Sets late or over budget; build doesn't match design; crew/vendor mismanagement.
- **LINE:** Reports to the production designer; manages set designers, construction coordinator, and department budget.

### Set Decorator
- **OWN:** Everything inside the set that isn't structure or hand-props — furniture, drapes, rugs, dressing. Makes a built space feel lived-in and true.
- **GOOD:** Dressing that tells character/period without a line of dialogue; sourced to budget; nothing anachronistic or generic.
- **FAIL:** Sterile or wrong-period dressing; continuity errors; over/under-dressed frames.
- **LINE:** Reports to the production designer; directs the lead man/set dressers (distinct from art director's construction side).

### Props Master
- **OWN:** Every object an actor handles — sourcing, continuity, function, and safety (including weapons). Owns hand-props from prep to wrap.
- **GOOD:** The right prop, in multiples, that works on cue; hero props built/sourced; weapons/effects safe; continuity tracked per take.
- **FAIL:** Missing/non-functional props; continuity breaks; unsafe weapon handling (a safety-critical failure).
- **LINE:** Reports to the production designer (sometimes the director for hero props); coordinates with scripty on continuity.

### Construction Coordinator
- **OWN:** Physically building the sets — carpentry, scenic, the build schedule and crew.
- **GOOD:** Sets built safely, on schedule, to spec, that survive shooting and strike cleanly.
- **FAIL:** Late builds; unsafe structures; over budget; poor scenic finish.
- **LINE:** Reports to the art director; runs carpenters, scenics, painters.

## 5. Costume, Hair & Makeup

### Costume Designer
- **OWN:** What every character wears — design, sourcing/building, and the through-line of costume as character across the whole story.
- **GOOD:** Costume that defines character and tracks arc/period; works with the DP on color/camera; multiples for stunts/continuity; to budget.
- **FAIL:** Costume that fights character or period; doesn't read on camera; no doubles for damage/stunts; over budget.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director; heads the costume supervisor and wardrobe crew.

### Costume Supervisor / Wardrobe
- **OWN:** Set execution and continuity of costume — fittings logistics, on-set dressing, damage/aging repeatability, garment tracking.
- **GOOD:** Right costume, right condition, every take; continuity photos; fast changes; garments maintained.
- **FAIL:** Continuity breaks; missing pieces; unrepeatable aging/blood.
- **LINE:** Reports to the costume designer.

### Hair Department Head / Makeup Department Head
- **OWN:** The character look of hair and of makeup — design, application, and continuity across the shoot.
- **GOOD:** Looks that suit character/period and hold under lights and time; continuity per scene; fast enough to make the call time.
- **FAIL:** Anachronistic or camera-wrong looks; continuity errors; slow chairs that push call times.
- **LINE:** Report to the director (design) and coordinate with the 2nd AD (schedule). Separate department heads for hair and for makeup.

### Special Effects (SFX) Makeup Artist / Prosthetics
- **OWN:** Physical practical character effects on the body — prosthetics, wounds, aging, creatures, blood.
- **GOOD:** Effects that read as real on camera and survive the shooting day; repeatable; applied within the schedule window.
- **FAIL:** Effects that look fake or degrade under lights; application that eats hours; not repeatable for continuity.
- **LINE:** Reports to the makeup department head or directly to the director/PD for major creature work. (Distinct from digital VFX and from physical SFX/pyro.)

## 6. Sound

### Production Sound Mixer
- **OWN:** Clean location audio — dialogue first, plus ambience and wild lines, recorded and mixed on set. Owns everything captured live.
- **GOOD:** Broadcast-clean dialogue that minimizes ADR; smart mic choices; flags noise problems before the take; organized sound reports.
- **FAIL:** Noisy/unusable dialogue forcing expensive ADR; missed wild lines/room tone; bad reports; doesn't warn about noise.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director/1st AD on set; hands audio to post sound. Heads the sound department (boom op, utility).

### Boom Operator
- **OWN:** Mic placement — keeping the boom optimally close to the source and out of frame/shadow, live, following the action.
- **GOOD:** Mic always in the sweet spot, never in frame, never casting shadow; anticipates actor movement; manages radio mics.
- **FAIL:** Boom in frame or shadow (ruins the take for camera); mic too far (thin dialogue); missed movement.
- **LINE:** Reports to the production sound mixer.

### Sound Designer (post)
- **OWN:** The created sonic world — every non-dialogue, non-music sound and its aesthetic. Designs how the film sounds.
- **GOOD:** A sound world that builds emotion and space invisibly; signature effects; supports picture without calling attention.
- **FAIL:** Generic or distracting effects; sonic world that fights tone; gaps and holes.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director/picture editor; hands to the re-recording mixer.

### Re-Recording Mixer (Dubbing Mixer)
- **OWN:** The final mix — balancing dialogue, music, and effects into the deliverable mix across all formats (stereo, 5.1, Atmos).
- **GOOD:** A mix where dialogue is always intelligible, music and effects breathe, and it translates from theater to phone; hits spec for every deliverable.
- **FAIL:** Buried dialogue; unbalanced stems; a mix that falls apart on small speakers; missed loudness spec.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director/supervising sound editor; delivers final mixed stems.

### ADR (Dialogue Replacement) & Foley
- **OWN:** ADR — re-recording dialogue in the studio to replace unusable production audio or add lines, matched to lip and performance. Foley — performing custom sync sound effects (footsteps, cloth, handling) to picture.
- **GOOD:** ADR indistinguishable from production sound; foley that makes the world tactile and syncs invisibly.
- **FAIL:** ADR that sounds "studio" and out of sync/space; foley that's off-sync or wrong-textured.
- **LINE:** Report to the supervising sound editor / sound designer.

## 7. Editorial & Picture Post

### Editor (Picture Editor)
- **OWN:** The cut — shot selection, timing, rhythm, and structure. The final assembly of story from footage. The "second writer" / "final rewrite."
- **GOOD:** Finds the best performance and the story's true rhythm; fixes script/shooting problems in the cut; serves emotion over showing off coverage; strong partnership with the director.
- **FAIL:** Flat pacing; wrong performance choices; cuts that break continuity/eyelines; can't kill darlings; loses the story in the coverage.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director (and, in TV, the showrunner, who holds final cut); receives dailies from editorial; hands locked picture to VFX/color/sound.

### Assistant Editor
- **OWN:** The edit's technical backbone — media management, syncing dailies, organizing bins, tracking VFX/versions, keeping the project shippable.
- **GOOD:** Flawless media/sync; everything findable; turnovers to VFX/sound/color clean and documented; the editor never fights the software or a missing clip.
- **FAIL:** Sync errors; lost/misorganized media; botched turnovers; project corruption.
- **LINE:** Reports to the editor; coordinates with the post supervisor and all post vendors.

### Colorist
- **OWN:** The final color and grade — matching shots, setting mood, and the definitive look of the finished image.
- **GOOD:** Seamless shot-matching across days/cameras; a grade that deepens tone and directs the eye; realizes the DP's intent; consistent across the whole piece.
- **FAIL:** Mismatched shots; grade that fights the story or crushes detail; inconsistent look; ignores DP/director intent.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director and DP; works from the DIT's on-set looks; delivers graded master.

### VFX Supervisor
- **OWN:** All visual effects — feasibility, methodology, and quality from prep through final comp. Decides how each effect is achieved and holds the bar.
- **GOOD:** Plans in pre-production so plates are shot VFX-ready; picks the right method (practical vs. digital) for cost/quality; effects that serve story and hold up; manages vendors to budget.
- **FAIL:** Unshootable plates (fixed expensively in post); effects that scream "CGI"; budget/schedule blowouts; effects that don't serve the story.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director/producer; starts in prep on set; heads VFX artists and outside houses.

### VFX Artists (compositors, CG, matte, roto, FX sim)
- **OWN:** The individual shot craft — compositing, 3D/CG, matte painting, rotoscoping, simulations that build the final image.
- **GOOD:** Photoreal integration (light, grain, edges, motion match) that's invisible; hits the brief and the deadline.
- **FAIL:** Obvious comps (bad edges, wrong light/grain); shots that don't match plates; missed deadlines.
- **LINE:** Report to the VFX supervisor / VFX house leads.

### Virtual Production Supervisor / Volume "Brain Bar" (LED-volume shows)
- **OWN:** The real-time in-camera-VFX (ICVFX) shoot — the LED wall/ceiling ("the Volume"), the Unreal Engine environments, camera tracking, and the frustum that keeps the background perspective-correct for the moving camera. The "brain bar" operators run and tune it live between takes.
- **GOOD:** Environments built and optimized *in prep* so they hold up in-camera; tracking locked to milliseconds (true parallax, no lag); real interactive light on the actors; the DP partly lighting with the wall itself.
- **FAIL:** Moiré and visible pixels in reflections/close-ups; tracking lag that breaks the parallax; using the volume where green screen or location was the right call; assets that weren't finished before the shoot day.
- **LINE:** A prep-heavy collaboration between the VFX supervisor, DP, and production designer; reports up to the director/producer. See `references/animation-vfx-sound.md` Part B for the full ICVFX workflow.

## 8. Music

### Composer
- **OWN:** The original score — themes, instrumentation, and emotional underscore written to picture.
- **GOOD:** Themes that carry character/story; music that guides emotion without telegraphing; works to locked picture and spotting notes; delivers to deadline.
- **FAIL:** Generic or on-the-nose score; music that fights the scene; late delivery; ignores the director's temp intent.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director; spots the film with director/editor; delivers stems to the re-recording mixer.

### Music Supervisor
- **OWN:** Licensed music — song selection, rights clearance, and budget for existing recordings. Owns needle-drops and their legality.
- **GOOD:** Songs that land emotionally and legally; cleared on time and budget; negotiates masters + publishing; alternatives ready when a clearance dies.
- **FAIL:** Uncleared music (a legal/distribution blocker); blown music budget; a signature song that falls through at the last minute.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director/producer; coordinates with editor and legal.

## 9. Cast, Casting, Stunts, Intimacy

### Casting Director
- **OWN:** The talent shortlist — finding, auditioning, and recommending actors for every role, plus negotiating deals with agents.
- **GOOD:** Surfaces the perfect and the unexpected-perfect actor; reads chemistry; deep knowledge of talent and rates; negotiates to budget.
- **FAIL:** Miscasting; missing an undiscovered better option; blown deals/quotes; poor chemistry reads.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director/producer; presents choices — the director/producer make the final call.

### Actors / Cast
- **OWN:** The performance — a truthful, repeatable, directable character take by take, on marks, matching continuity.
- **GOOD:** Truthful and consistent across coverage; hits marks and eyelines without going wooden; takes direction; repeatable for editing.
- **FAIL:** Inconsistent performance across takes (unusable in the cut); missing marks; unrepeatable; undirectable.
- **LINE:** Report to the director; ATL principals.

### Stunt Coordinator
- **OWN:** Action safety and design — choreographing and rigging every stunt so it looks real and no one gets hurt. Safety is the deliverable.
- **GOOD:** Action that reads as dangerous while being controlled; zero injuries; performers/rigs matched to cast; works with camera for the best angle.
- **FAIL:** Injury (the defining failure); action that looks fake or under-cranked; poor camera coverage of the stunt.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director/1st AD; heads stunt performers and doubles; coordinates with key grip on rigging.

### Intimacy Coordinator
- **OWN:** Consent and choreography for intimate/nude/simulated-sex scenes — the actor advocate who choreographs intimacy and enforces agreed boundaries. (SAG-AFTRA has formal standards.)
- **GOOD:** Actors safe, informed, and continuously consenting; intimacy choreographed like a stunt to the director's vision; closed-set and modesty protocols enforced; nudity riders honored.
- **FAIL:** Boundary violations; unagreed changes on the day; actors pressured; a scene that harms performers or exposes production to liability.
- **LINE:** Liaison between cast and production; reports to the director/producer; coordinates like a department head for intimate scenes.

## 10. Post Management & On-Set Support

### Post-Production Supervisor
- **OWN:** The post pipeline — scheduling, budgeting, and coordinating every post vendor (edit, VFX, color, sound, music) to deliverables and dates.
- **GOOD:** All vendors moving in sync to a delivery calendar; nothing dropped between houses; budget and specs tracked; delivery hits the date and the platform's tech spec.
- **FAIL:** Missed delivery; vendor bottlenecks; turnover chaos; blown post budget; failed QC on deliverables.
- **LINE:** Reports to the producer/EP (and a studio/network post VP on large jobs); oversees post coordinator, assistant editors, and all post vendors.

### Location Manager
- **OWN:** Where it shoots — scouting, securing, permitting, and managing every real-world location and the community/legal relationship around it.
- **GOOD:** Locations that fit the design and the logistics (parking, power, sound, access); permits and neighbor relations airtight; a backup for weather/fallthrough.
- **FAIL:** Lost location at the last minute; permit/neighbor problems that halt shooting; a site that can't hold the crew/gear.
- **LINE:** Reports to the UPM/line producer and production designer; coordinates with the 1st AD.

### Transportation Coordinator
- **OWN:** Moving everything — cast, crew, and equipment vehicles, drivers, and the transport schedule.
- **GOOD:** Everyone and everything on location on time; fleet maintained and legal; picture vehicles ready on cue.
- **FAIL:** Late arrivals that push the day; vehicle failures; picture-car problems.
- **LINE:** Reports to the UPM/line producer; runs the drivers/captains.

### Craft Services vs. Catering
- **OWN:** Catering owns the hot meals (the scheduled sit-down that resets the clock). Craft services owns continuous on-set snacks, coffee, and hydration between meals.
- **GOOD:** Fed, watered, and energized crew; dietary needs met; meals on time (late meals trigger penalties). Crew morale runs partly through the food.
- **FAIL:** Meal penalties from late catering; a hungry, low-morale crew; unsafe food handling; dietary needs ignored.
- **LINE:** Report to the UPM/production; a hidden lever on schedule and morale.

### Production Assistants (PAs)
- **OWN:** Whatever keeps the day moving — the general labor and communication layer (set PAs lock up and relay; office PAs run logistics). The entry-level connective tissue.
- **GOOD:** Anticipate needs; lock up a set silently; relay accurate info fast; reliable and unflappable; the AD department's eyes and legs.
- **FAIL:** Dropped tasks; bad lockups (background noise/people in shot); garbled relays; unreliable.
- **LINE:** Set PAs report to the 2nd/2nd 2nd AD; office PAs to the production coordinator/UPM.

### Production Coordinator
- **OWN:** The production office — the paperwork, communication, travel, and logistics hub connecting every department.
- **GOOD:** Every department gets info, gear, and travel on time; the office never drops a request; the connective tissue between office and floor.
- **FAIL:** Miscommunication between departments; travel/logistics failures; lost paperwork.
- **LINE:** Reports to the UPM/line producer; directs office PAs.

## 12. Animation Department
A parallel crew, because animation has no live-action shoot — the "camera," "actors," and "lighting" are all authored roles. (Full craft in `references/animation-vfx-sound.md` Part A.)

### Animation Director / Supervising Animator
- **OWN:** The performance and motion vision — casting shots to animators, giving performance notes, and holding the acting and timing bar across the film.
- **GOOD:** Consistent, thinking, *weighted* performances that serve story; notes that push past the reference; the 12 principles applied deliberately.
- **FAIL:** Floaty/weightless motion; twinning; performances that signal without a thought behind them; inconsistent character acting.
- **LINE:** Reports to the director; leads the animators. (In anime, the *sakuga*/animation director also corrects key drawings for on-model consistency.)

### Story Artist (Storyboard Artist)
- **OWN:** Translating the script into boarded, staged, cut sequences — pitching and effectively *rewriting* scenes through drawing. A filmmaker, not an illustrator.
- **GOOD:** Clear staging and cutting; performance beats that read; sequences that survive into the reel.
- **FAIL:** Pretty panels that don't cut; unclear staging; boards that ignore screen direction or the scene's turn.
- **LINE:** Reports to the head of story / director; feeds the editorial **story reel**.

### Visual Development / Character Designer / Art Director
- **OWN:** The film's visual identity — character/model sheets, color scripts, environment and prop design.
- **GOOD:** Designs that are appealing, on-theme, *and buildable/riggable*; a color script that maps the film's emotional temperature.
- **FAIL:** Off-model inconsistency; designs the pipeline can't realize, discovered after rigging starts.
- **LINE:** Under the production designer / art director; hands off to modeling and layout.

### Layout Artist (the "virtual DP")
- **OWN:** Camera, lens, staging, and blocking in the (2D or 3D) shot — cinematography without a physical camera.
- **GOOD:** Motivated framing and moves; readable staging; coverage that cuts.
- **FAIL:** Unmotivated moves; flat staging; shots that don't edit together.
- **LINE:** Works from boards; hands blocked shots to animation.

### Modeler · Rigger · Lighter · Compositor (3D/CG pipeline)
- **OWN:** **Modeler** — the geometry; **Rigger** — the animator's puppet (skeleton, controls, deformers, facial rig); **Lighter** — mood/depth/focus with virtual lights; **Compositor** — assembling render passes into the final image.
- **GOOD:** An invisible rig that never fights the animator; lighting matched to intent; comp that integrates every pass seamlessly.
- **FAIL:** A rig that fights every pose; lighting with no mood or continuity; a comp that reads as separate layers.
- **LINE:** The dependency chain — model → rig → animate → light → render → comp — under the relevant supervisor. (2D equivalents: key animator → inbetweener → clean-up → ink & paint.)

## Format-Specific Divergences

- **Features:** Deepest hierarchy; director holds creative authority; full ATL/BTL split with union crews.
- **TV / Streaming drama:** **Showrunner** holds final creative and budgetary authority; directors are episodic visitors; heavy writers' room; continuity across episodes is paramount; the EP title usually means senior writer, not financier.
- **Documentary:** Radically compressed — director often DP/producer/editor; small crews; sound and access matter more than lighting; "producer" may mean field-producer/logistics; editorial is where the story is actually written.
- **Commercials:** Agency and client sit above the director; the brief and boards are contractual; a production-company producer plus agency producer; short, high-crew, high-budget-per-day shoots; the edit serves the brand.
- **Music videos:** Director-driven and visually experimental; commissioner/label/artist above the director; compressed crews; performance + concept coverage; often heavy on grip/electric and VFX relative to run time.
- **Animation:** No live-action shoot — the whole camera/lighting/performance crew is replaced by the Animation Department (§12). "Editing" happens up front in the **story reel/animatic**, not in post; the director gives performance notes to animators over days per shot, not takes on a set. Stop-motion (Laika) and anime (production-committee financing, "limited animation") each shift the crew again.
- **Virtual production:** A hybrid — a live-action crew *plus* the Volume "brain bar," real-time engine artists, and prep-built environments; VFX moves into pre-production and the DP lights partly with the LED wall.
