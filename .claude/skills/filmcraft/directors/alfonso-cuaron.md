# Director Sub-Skill: Alfonso Cuarón
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in Cuarón's mode — or to answer "how would Cuarón do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Journeys through crisis toward rebirth — a vulnerable person (child, mother, pregnant refugee) navigating a hostile world; social class and the invisible labor of others (*Roma*, *Y Tu Mamá También*); memory, motherhood, and the political embedded in the intimate. Survival and grace under catastrophe.

**Working method.** Master of the long take used for *immersion*, not showoff — developed with DP Emmanuel Lubezki. Extreme technical planning to achieve it: *Children of Men*'s car-ambush needed a custom rig (a specially-built car with a removable roof and rotating mount) and months of choreography so one unbroken shot could carry the scene; *Gravity* used a robotic-arm "Light Box" (an LED cube) to fake weightlessness and control light over long CG takes. On *Roma* he shot digitally in luminous black-and-white, operated the camera himself, reconstructed his own 1970s Mexico City block from memory, and fed a non-actor lead information day-by-day.

**Signature (spottable).** The immersive long take with layered foreground/background (a whole world blocked into one shot). Deep-focus wide compositions; the lateral tracking shot following a character through a charged environment. Realism heightened by immaculate sound design (the Atmos street-life of *Roma*). Natural-feeling light. Water, birth, and death imagery. Social background made foreground. Technical invention in service of empathy.

**Defining films.** *Y Tu Mamá También* (2001) · *Children of Men* (2006) · *Gravity* (2013) · *Roma* (2018); also *A Little Princess* (1995), *Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban* (2004).

**Key collaborators.** DP Emmanuel "Chivo" Lubezki (his essential partner; *Children of Men*, *Gravity*); his son Jonás Cuarón (co-writer, *Gravity*); sound designers Skip Lievsay / Sergio Díaz (*Roma*); the "Three Amigos" peer circle (Guillermo del Toro, Alejandro González Iñárritu); producer Gabriela Rodríguez. He edited *Roma* himself.

**The one lesson to steal.** The long take is worth its enormous logistical cost when it makes the audience *inhabit* the scene in real time — but only if every technical choice serves immersion and empathy, not spectacle. Reconstructing lived memory with obsessive specificity yields uncanny authenticity.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** The virtuoso long-take/technology can overshadow the drama — *Gravity* is often called a technical marvel with a thin script. *Roma*'s beauty prompted debate about whether it aestheticizes and slightly sidelines its indigenous domestic-worker subject (seen through the affluent family's nostalgia). The set-pieces can become the point.

Sources: *American Cinematographer* deep-dives with Lubezki on *Children of Men* and *Gravity* · the *Children of Men* long-take featurettes · Cuarón interviews around *Roma* (DGA Quarterly, Film Comment) · Framestore's *Gravity* VFX breakdowns.
