# Director Sub-Skill: David Fincher
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in Fincher's mode — or to answer "how would Fincher do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Obsession itself — process, systems, and control; the procedural pursuit of an elusive truth or person (*Zodiac*, *The Social Network*, *Mindhunter*); alienated men and toxic modern masculinity; the seductiveness and cost of perfectionism. The darkness of human behavior rendered with clinical precision.

**Working method.** The digital-precision perfectionist — famous for 50–100+ takes, wearing actors down until they stop *performing* and behavioral truth leaks through. Early adopter and pioneer of digital tools: shoots digital, does extensive *invisible* VFX (stitching takes, set extensions, removing breath, compositing the best of several performances). Storyboards/previsualizes precisely; locks the frame, minimal handheld. Controls color and contrast obsessively in the DI. Rehearses via on-set repetition.

**Signature (spottable).** Locked-off, smooth, motivated camera — often a slow push-in; the "impossible" digital move (the *Panic Room* travel through a coffee-pot handle). Desaturated greenish-amber low-key palette, deep shadow with detail — darkness you can still read. Immaculate framing and dialogue-rhythm cutting (*The Social Network*'s overlapping-talk pace). Invisible VFX. Kinetic title sequences. Trent Reznor & Atticus Ross's pulsing electronic dread.

**Defining films.** *Se7en* (1995) · *Fight Club* (1999) · *Zodiac* (2007) · *The Social Network* (2010) · *The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo* (2011) · *Gone Girl* (2014); TV *Mindhunter*.

**Key collaborators.** DPs Harris Savides (*Zodiac*), Jeff Cronenweth (*Fight Club*, *Social Network*, *Gone Girl*), Erik Messerschmidt (*Mank*); composers Trent Reznor & Atticus Ross; writers Aaron Sorkin, Gillian Flynn; editors Kirk Baxter & Angus Wall; producer Ceán Chaffin.

**The one lesson to steal.** Repetition to exhaustion strips performance down to truth — and digital tools let you assemble the perfect take from many. Control tone through color, contrast, and camera stillness; let precision itself become the style.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Chilliness — the clinical control can leave films emotionally airless, more admirable than moving; a fascination with serial-killer/dark-male material that flirts with the things it critiques (*Fight Club*'s reception). The take-count perfectionism is criticized as ego and as brutal on collaborators; a supreme technician sometimes in search of deeper feeling.

Sources: Fincher's DVD commentaries (esp. *Zodiac*, *The Social Network* — very technical) · *David Fincher: Interviews* · *American Cinematographer* on Cronenweth/Savides/Messerschmidt · the *Voir* essays.
