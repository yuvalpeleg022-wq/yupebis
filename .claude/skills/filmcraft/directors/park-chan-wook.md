# Director Sub-Skill: Park Chan-wook
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in Park's mode — or to answer "how would Park Chan-wook do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Revenge and its moral rot — vengeance that consumes the avenger; guilt, complicity, and moral ambiguity; desire and transgression (forbidden love, incest); the tension between beauty and brutality. Redemption sought and denied.

**Working method.** A meticulous storyboarder and shot-designer — highly controlled, choreographed compositions. Started as a film critic, so intensely cinephilic and formally self-aware. Builds set-pieces around a single audacious idea (the *Oldboy* corridor fight as a side-scrolling single take). Uses production design and color as narrative *code*. Co-writes with a steady partner and works with a consistent craft team.

**Signature (spottable).** The oner action set-piece (the *Oldboy* hallway). Ornate, symmetrical, wallpapered production design (*Stoker*, *The Handmaiden*). Sudden tonal whiplash — black comedy inside horror inside melodrama. Elaborate camera moves and match cuts across time and space (the *Stoker* hair-to-grass transition). Lush color and fetishistic texture. Structural trickery — reversals and dual perspectives retelling the same events (*The Handmaiden*'s three-part Rashomon structure). Shocking violence rendered beautifully.

**Defining films.** The Vengeance Trilogy — *Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance* (2002), *Oldboy* (2003), *Lady Vengeance* (2005) · *Thirst* (2009) · *Stoker* (2013) · *The Handmaiden* (2016) · *Decision to Leave* (2022, Cannes Best Director).

**Key collaborators.** DP Chung Chung-hoon (his long-running cinematographer); co-writer Chung Seo-kyung (his key writing partner, esp. *The Handmaiden*, *Decision to Leave*); actors Choi Min-sik (*Oldboy*), Song Kang-ho, Kim Min-hee, Tang Wei.

**The one lesson to steal.** Storyboard the audacious set-piece to the frame — a single perfectly-designed sequence (the corridor fight) can define a whole film. And structure as revelation: retelling the same events from a new POV reframes everything.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Style over feeling — the immaculate design and shock value can read as cold, calculated, "provocation as decoration"; transgression as aesthetic thrill more than moral inquiry. Some find the tonal whiplash flippant and the twists mechanical; the beauty sometimes anaesthetizes the horror it depicts.

Sources: Park interviews around *Oldboy* and *The Handmaiden* (Film Comment, Sight & Sound) · the *Oldboy* DVD commentaries · Chung Chung-hoon in *American Cinematographer*.
