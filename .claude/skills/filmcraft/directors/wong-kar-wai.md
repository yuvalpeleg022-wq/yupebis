# Director Sub-Skill: Wong Kar-wai
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in Wong's mode — or to answer "how would Wong Kar-wai do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Time, memory, and missed connection — love that fails to synchronize. Expiration dates (the pineapple cans of *Chungking Express*), clocks, the exact minute. Longing for people physically near but temporally/emotionally out of reach. He returns compulsively to the early-1960s Shanghai-émigré Hong Kong of his childhood (*In the Mood for Love*, *2046*, *Days of Being Wild*).

**Working method.** Shoots without a finished script — writes the night before or on set, discovers the film in the edit. *In the Mood for Love* shot ~15 months and was reconceived entirely; *2046* took ~5 years. Shoots enormous footage and finds the movie later (whole performances get cut). Uses music on set as a writing tool, to set rhythm and mood. Works with the same core crew as a repertory family.

**Signature (spottable).** Step-printing / smeared slow-motion (blurred motion against a near-still figure). Saturated, expressionist color — reds and greens, neon, filtered light. Frames blocked by foreground obstructions — shooting past doorframes, mirrors, grilles, glass, so we *spy*. Cramped claustrophobic interiors; voiceover interior monologue; repetition with variation (the same corridor, the same song). Costume as psychology (Maggie Cheung's cheongsams marking time). Elliptical jump-cut editing.

**Defining films.** *Days of Being Wild* (1990) · *Chungking Express* (1994) · *Fallen Angels* (1995) · *Happy Together* (1997) · *In the Mood for Love* (2000) · *2046* (2004).

**Key collaborators.** DP Christopher Doyle (the smeared/handheld look) and Mark Lee Ping-bing (*ITMFL*); William Chang Suk-ping (editor *and* production/costume designer — arguably co-author); actors Tony Leung Chiu-wai and Maggie Cheung; composer Shigeru Umebayashi ("Yumeji's Theme"), plus Nat King Cole and Latin standards.

**The one lesson to steal.** Mood and rhythm can carry narrative — withhold plot and still hold an audience if the sensory/emotional field is precise. Costume, set, and music aren't decoration; they're the storytelling instrument.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** The no-script method produces incoherence and self-indulgence when it misfires (*2046* is diffuse; *My Blueberry Nights* thin and mannered). His women can be objects of male reverie rather than subjects; the style can curdle into perfume-ad prettiness. The process burns actors and budgets.

Sources: Christopher Doyle's on-set diaries (esp. the *Happy Together* diary) · Stephen Teo, *Wong Kar-wai: Auteur of Time* (BFI) · Criterion essays for *In the Mood for Love* and *Chungking Express*.
