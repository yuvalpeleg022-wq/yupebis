# Director Sub-Skill: Martin Scorsese
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md.*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Catholic guilt, sin, and the impossibility of redemption. Men who want grace but are wired for violence and pride; the seduction of power and its bill coming due. He shows the glamour of the gangster/wiseguy fully so the fall lands — never a sermon, always a temptation you feel first.

**Working method.** Meticulous storyboarded shot design, but he locks the camera down for improvised actor scenes because (per editor Thelma Schoonmaker) you can't cut through a moving camera during improv. Schoonmaker stays off-set to keep a "cold perspective," reacting only to what's on film. Editing runs ~9 months with Scorsese present daily. Deep film-history literacy feeds every choice.

**Signature (spottable).** Kinetic motivated camera — whip pans, dolly-ins, the Copacabana Steadicam oner in *Goodfellas*; freeze-frames and voiceover; rock/pop needle-drops used ironically against violence (Layla over corpses); rapid smash- and jump-cuts compressing time; slow-motion to isolate dread; direct address to camera.

**Defining films.** *Taxi Driver* (1976) — urban alienation as a slow detonation · *Raging Bull* (1980) — self-destruction as brutal black-and-white opera; Schoonmaker's edit is the textbook · *Goodfellas* (1990) — the definitive rise-and-fall rhythm machine · *Casino* (1995) — excess and its accounting · *The Departed* (2006) — his Best Director Oscar; identity and betrayal · *The Wolf of Wall Street* (2013) — greed as manic comedy · *The Irishman* (2019) — the gangster mythology aged into regret and silence.

**Key collaborators.** Editor Thelma Schoonmaker (every film since *Raging Bull*). Actors Robert De Niro (early era), Leonardo DiCaprio (modern era), Joe Pesci. DPs Michael Ballhaus, later Rodrigo Prieto. Writer Paul Schrader on the seminal ones. Music supervision by Robbie Robertson.

**The one lesson to steal.** Let the audience *enjoy* the sin. Seduce first; the moral weight only lands if you made the temptation real.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Charged with glamorizing the violence and machismo he means to critique — audiences cheer the wiseguys. Women are frequently decorative or victims. Late films run long (*The Irishman*, *Killers of the Flower Moon*, ~3.5 hrs each). Returns to the gangster well often enough that critics call it a comfort zone.
