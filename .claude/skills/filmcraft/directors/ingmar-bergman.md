# Director Sub-Skill: Ingmar Bergman
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in Bergman's mode — or to answer "how would Bergman do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** The silence of God and the terror of death; faith, doubt, and the impossibility of communication between people (marriage as combat). The face as the landscape of the soul. Art vs. life; the artist as vampire/fraud (*Persona*, *Hour of the Wolf*).

**Working method.** Wrote all his own screenplays (often first as prose). Ran a theatre company (the Royal Dramatic Theatre) and treated film like theatre — long rehearsal, actor-centric. Shot on his home island, Fårö, with a small "family" crew and cast. Disciplined and on-budget (unlike Kurosawa). Exacting about faces: pushed the camera into extreme close-up and *held*. Collaborated intensely — creatively and romantically — with his lead actresses.

**Signature (spottable).** The sustained facial close-up and the two-faces-merging composition (*Persona*). Chamber-drama structure — few characters, one location, dialogue as duel. Dreams and reality bleeding together; direct-to-camera monologue. Stark high-contrast black-and-white (Sven Nykvist's soft, sourceless Nordic light), then luminous crimson color in *Cries and Whispers* ("the inside of the soul"). Clocks, mirrors, theatrical framing.

**Defining films.** *The Seventh Seal* (1957) · *Wild Strawberries* (1957) · the faith trilogy *Through a Glass Darkly* / *Winter Light* / *The Silence* (1961–63) · *Persona* (1966) · *Cries and Whispers* (1972) · *Scenes from a Marriage* (1973) · *Fanny and Alexander* (1982).

**Key collaborators.** DPs Gunnar Fischer (early) and above all Sven Nykvist (the light); actors Max von Sydow, Liv Ullmann, Bibi Andersson, Ingrid Thulin, Erland Josephson, Gunnar Björnstrand, Harriet Andersson.

**The one lesson to steal.** Trust the human face — hold on it long past comfort and let performance, not cutting, carry the scene. Chamber-scale limitation (few people, one room) intensifies rather than shrinks drama.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Airless, self-serious "arthouse gloom" — the anguish can feel relentless and hermetic. He later admitted the God-torment was partly a lapsed-pastor's-son psychodrama. His treatment of women in his personal life (and the vampiric-artist self-critique) is fraught. Can be talky and static.

Sources: Bergman, *The Magic Lantern* and *Images: My Life in Film* · Sven Nykvist, *Reverence for Light* · Peter Cowie, *Ingmar Bergman: A Critical Biography*.
