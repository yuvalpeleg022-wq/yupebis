# Director Sub-Skill: Agnès Varda
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in Varda's mode — or to answer "how would Varda do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Looking closely at the overlooked — gleaners, widows, the marginal, the discarded; the interplay of documentary and fiction; feminism and the female subject; and, increasingly, memory, aging, and her own life as material.

**Working method.** Coined **"cinécriture"** (cine-writing) — a film is authored like writing, every element (framing, editing, sound, movement) a word. Came from still photography, so composed like a photographer. Blended documentary and fiction fluidly and put *herself* in her films. Worked small, curious, improvisatory, following real people. Late career embraced the DV camera for intimacy and mobility (*The Gleaners and I* is a manifesto for the small digital camera), then installation art and the collaborative *Faces Places*.

**Signature (spottable).** The essay-film first person — her voice, her hands, her presence. Playful digressions and formal conceits (the "one hand filming the other"; *Cléo* unfolding in near-real time as a woman waits for medical results). Warm curiosity and wit; heart-shaped potatoes; self-portraiture. Mixing 16mm/35mm/DV; tableaux referencing painting. A feminist reframing of who gets to be looked at.

**Defining films.** *Cléo from 5 to 7* (1962) · *Le Bonheur* (1965) · *Vagabond / Sans toit ni loi* (1985) · *The Gleaners and I* (2000) · *The Beaches of Agnès* (2008) · *Faces Places* (2017, with JR).

**Key collaborators.** Her husband Jacques Demy (creative dialogue; subject of her *Jacquot de Nantes*); artist JR (*Faces Places*); actresses Corinne Marchand (*Cléo*) and Sandrine Bonnaire (*Vagabond*); the Left Bank group (with Chris Marker, Alain Resnais). She often edited and shaped her own films.

**The one lesson to steal.** The small, cheap, handheld camera is a feature, not a compromise — intimacy, first-person authorship, and curiosity beat production value. The maker's presence in the frame can be the film's honesty.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** The essayistic looseness can feel slight, meandering, or twee (the whimsy divides viewers). Some fiction features (*Le Bonheur*) are beautiful but morally cool in ways critics found evasive. Long underrated commercially; her influence outran her box office.

Sources: Varda, *Varda par Agnès* (book and film) · the Criterion box-set essays · Kelley Conway, *Agnès Varda* (Contemporary Film Directors).
