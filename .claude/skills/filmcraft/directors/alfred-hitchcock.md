# Director Sub-Skill: Alfred Hitchcock
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in Hitchcock's mode — or to answer "how would Hitchcock do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Guilt, suspicion, and the *transfer of guilt* — the ordinary man wrongly accused; voyeurism and the audience's complicity in looking (*Rear Window*, *Psycho*); control and desire, especially the fixation on the cool blonde. Order disrupted by hidden chaos.

**Working method.** "The film is finished before shooting begins" — storyboarded and pre-visualized so completely the shoot was near-mechanical (he claimed to be bored on set). Built set-pieces backward from the desired audience *sensation*. Controlled the audience's knowledge and POV to the frame. Worked closely with writers on structure; his "actors are cattle" was provocation about not indulging improvisation. (Bill Krohn's *Hitchcock at Work* shows the process was more flexible than the myth.)

**Signature (spottable).** Subjective POV editing — cut between a character looking and what they see (the Kuleshov effect weaponized). The dolly-zoom / "Vertigo shot" (track out + zoom in) he invented for *Vertigo*. Bravura long takes (*Rope*'s apparent single shot; the *Notorious* crane down to the key). The MacGuffin (the plot pretext that doesn't matter). Set-pieces in famous public spaces. Bernard Herrmann's scores. The director cameo. Audience-aligned dread.

**Defining films.** *Notorious* (1946) · *Rope* (1948) · *Strangers on a Train* (1951) · *Rear Window* (1954) · *Vertigo* (1958) · *North by Northwest* (1959) · *Psycho* (1960) · *The Birds* (1963); British-period *The 39 Steps* (1935), *The Lady Vanishes* (1938).

**Key collaborators.** Composer Bernard Herrmann (until their bitter split); title/consultant Saul Bass; writers Ernest Lehman (*NxNW*), John Michael Hayes (*Rear Window*), Joseph Stefano (*Psycho*); DP Robert Burks; editor George Tomasini; costume designer Edith Head; stars James Stewart, Cary Grant, Grace Kelly.

**The one lesson to steal.** Suspense = giving the audience information the characters lack, then controlling POV so viewers *feel* rather than watch. Surprise is 15 seconds; suspense (the audience knowing about the bomb) is 15 minutes. Solve the film on paper first.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** The control-to-death approach can flatten performance into cold engineering. His documented obsession with and mistreatment of actresses (Tippi Hedren's account of *The Birds*/*Marnie*) is central to reassessing him; the misogyny is in the work, not only the life. Late-career decline (*Topaz*, *Torn Curtain*).

Sources: [Truffaut, *Hitchcock/Truffaut*] · Bill Krohn, *Hitchcock at Work* · Patrick McGilligan, *Alfred Hitchcock: A Life in Darkness and Light* · Donald Spoto, *The Dark Side of Genius*.
