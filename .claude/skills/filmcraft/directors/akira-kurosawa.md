# Director Sub-Skill: Akira Kurosawa
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in Kurosawa's mode — or to answer "how would Kurosawa do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** The individual's moral responsibility inside violent, corrupt, or indifferent systems — bushido tested against reality; heroism and its cost; the unknowability of truth (*Rashomon*); compassion vs. cynicism (the doctor/mentor figures of *Ikiru*, *Red Beard*, *Drunken Angel*).

**Working method.** Trained as a painter — storyboarded/painted his shots (the late films have full-color storyboards). Pioneered **multi-camera long-lens shooting** (three cameras rolling simultaneously) so actors performed continuously and he could cut on real energy. Obsessive control of the elements — waited for the right clouds, used ink-black rain, real fire. Made actors live in costume to break it in. Edited daily during production (one of cinema's great cutters). Wrote with a team of co-writers drafting the same scenes in parallel, then compared.

**Signature (spottable).** Dynamic weather as emotion — rain, wind, mud, fog, heat haze (the climactic *Seven Samurai* downpour battle). Axial cuts, cut-on-motion. Telephoto compression and multi-plane staging. The wipe transition (his signature optical). Explosive movement against sudden stillness. Bold graphic compositions; color-coded armies in *Ran* and *Kagemusha*. The "stolen" long-lens performance intensity.

**Defining films.** *Rashomon* (1950) · *Ikiru* (1952) · *Seven Samurai* (1954) · *Throne of Blood* (1957) · *Yojimbo* (1961) · *High and Low* (1963) · *Red Beard* (1965); late color epics *Kagemusha* (1980), *Ran* (1985).

**Key collaborators.** Toshiro Mifune (16 films, the volcanic star) and Takashi Shimura; composers Fumio Hayasaka, Masaru Sato, Toru Takemitsu (*Ran*); co-writers Shinobu Hashimoto, Hideo Oguni, Ryuzo Kikushima; DPs Asakazu Nakai and Kazuo Miyagawa (*Rashomon*'s light-through-trees).

**The one lesson to steal.** Multi-camera long-lens coverage captures uninterrupted, unfakeable performance energy — and weather is dramaturgy, not backdrop. Also the writing discipline: multiple writers drafting the same scenes at once, then choosing the best.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Didacticism and sentimentality — the humanist message sometimes underlined too hard (*Red Beard*, late *Dreams*). A control-tyrant ("Tenno," the Emperor): expensive, uncompromising, near-unemployable in 1970s Japan (attempted suicide in 1971 after *Dodesukaden* flopped). His women are often marginal.

Sources: Kurosawa, *Something Like an Autobiography* · Donald Richie, *The Films of Akira Kurosawa* · Stephen Prince, *The Warrior's Camera* · Chris Marker's *AK*.
