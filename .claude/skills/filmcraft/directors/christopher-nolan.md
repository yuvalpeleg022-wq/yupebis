# Director Sub-Skill: Christopher Nolan
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in Nolan's mode — or to answer "how would Nolan do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Time as a manipulable, subjective substance — memory that lies (*Memento*), dreams nested in dreams (*Inception*), relativity as plot engine (*Interstellar*), inverted time (*Tenet*), braided simultaneous timelines (*Dunkirk*). Under the puzzle-box is a moral one: how a person lives with guilt, obsession, and the gap between what they know and what's true. Treats the audience as intelligent adults who should be trusted to keep up.

**Working method.** Writes alone (early films with brother Jonathan); guards scripts obsessively (red-cover, no-copy sets). Militantly pro-film, anti-digital — calls IMAX the best format ever invented. Practical-first effects: CGI only to *enhance* something photographed, never to originate ("if it's created from no physical elements it's going to feel like animation"). Real spinning hallway, real 747 crashed for *Tenet*, IMAX cameras on Spitfires. Stood by the camera for years, no video village. Tight fast shoots, minimal coverage because he pre-decides the cut.

**Signature (spottable).** Cross-cutting parallel timelines converging on one climax; escalating drone-and-pulse scores (the *Inception* "BRAAAM," the *Dunkirk* Shepard-tone ticking clock that feels forever-rising); aspect-ratio switching mid-film when IMAX kicks in; cold desaturated palettes; expository dialogue delivered on the move; a ticking-clock structural spine in almost every film.

**Defining films.** *Memento* (2000) — reverse-chronology built the whole thesis in miniature · *The Dark Knight* (2008) — comic-book film raised to crime epic; Ledger's Joker as pure chaos · *Inception* (2010) — the puzzle-box at blockbuster scale · *Interstellar* (2014) — hard-science relativity fused to father-daughter melodrama · *Dunkirk* (2017) — near-wordless three-timeline war-as-experience · *Tenet* (2020) — his most mechanically ambitious and coldest · *Oppenheimer* (2023) — the method scaled to a dialogue-driven biopic; his Best Director win.

**Key collaborators.** DPs Wally Pfister (through *Dark Knight Rises*) then Hoyte van Hoytema (*Interstellar* on). Editors Lee Smith (through *Dunkirk*) then Jennifer Lame (*Tenet*, *Oppenheimer*). Composers Hans Zimmer then Ludwig Göransson (*Tenet*, *Oppenheimer*). Recurring cast: Michael Caine, Cillian Murphy, Tom Hardy, Kenneth Branagh. Producer/wife Emma Thomas.

**The one lesson to steal.** Structure *is* content. Choose how time is delivered to the audience as deliberately as what happens — the ordering of information is the emotional experience.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Emotionally chilly and expositionally over-clocked — characters explain the film's rules to each other. Thin female characters (the dead-wife motive recurs). Sound mixes bury dialogue under score. The cleverness can become an end in itself: mechanics you admire rather than people you feel.
