# Director Sub-Skill: Mani Ratnam
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md.*
*Roja · Bombay · Dil Se · Nayakan · Guru*

Redefined the range and depth of Tamil cinema; one of India's great craftsman-auteurs. He tells intimate love stories set against the fault lines of Indian politics — Kashmir terrorism (*Roja*), the Bombay communal riots (*Bombay*), insurgency and obsession (*Dil Se*) — the loose "terrorism trilogy" that made his name.

**Signature:** visual — obsessive **rim-lighting**, silhouette, rain, painterly framing, plus experimentation in colour, camera movement, and cutting that made his films look unlike anyone else's. Crucially, he **discovered A.R. Rahman** (debuting him on *Roja*, 1992) and has used him since — their song sequences are among Indian cinema's most beautiful, treating the political backdrop as gorgeously as the romance ("make what came before the riots as beautiful as possible").

**Collaborators:** composer A.R. Rahman; DPs Santosh Sivan, P.C. Sreeram, Rajiv Menon; major stars across Tamil and Hindi.

**The one lesson:** politics lands hardest through a small, human love story — and beauty is not a betrayal of serious subject matter, it's the delivery system.

**Weakness:** aestheticism can outrun substance; his political films sometimes soften hard realities into liberal both-sides resolution, and his gorgeous surfaces occasionally paper over thin or convenient plotting.
