# Director Sub-Skill: Steven Spielberg
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md.*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Wonder, childhood, and the fractured family — the child confronting the awesome (aliens, dinosaurs, sharks, history) and the absent or failing father figure, recurring across four decades. Even his darkest films (*Schindler's List*, *Munich*) hunt for a moral center and the possibility of decency.

**Working method.** Blocking and staging mastery — uses stand-ins ("second team") to "math out" the physics of a shot: lens height, dolly speed, the exact frame a character crosses. Often opens on a close-up of a key prop as a subtextual anchor. Fast and instinctive on set, shoots efficiently, pre-visualizes complex sequences meticulously (the shark, the raptors) — frequently hiding enormous logistical complexity inside a single flowing take.

**Signature (spottable).** The "Spielberg Oner" — a complex continuous take folding several shots' worth of angles and blocking into one move. The "Spielberg Face" — hold on a character's awe/fear, *then* cut to what they see, with Williams' score putting it over the top. Slow push-ins on reaction; backlight and lens flares; kids at eye level; the reveal delayed and delayed.

**Defining films.** *Jaws* (1975) — invented the summer blockbuster; withholding the shark taught the world suspense · *Close Encounters* (1977) — the purest distillation of Spielbergian wonder · *E.T.* (1982) — childhood, loss, belief · *Raiders of the Lost Ark* (1981) — action-adventure craft at its peak · *Jurassic Park* (1993) — the CGI/practical hybrid that changed VFX · *Schindler's List* (1993) — his artistic and moral summit; first Best Director Oscar · *Saving Private Ryan* (1998) — visceral war realism; second Oscar.

**Key collaborators.** Composer John Williams — 40+ years, cinema's most famous director-composer partnership. DPs Janusz Kamiński (every film since *Schindler's List*) and Allen Daviau early. Editor Michael Kahn. Producers Kathleen Kennedy, Frank Marshall.

**The one lesson to steal.** Direct the audience's *emotion*, not just the information — hold on the face before you reveal the thing, and let the reaction teach the viewer how to feel.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Sentimentality — "Spielbergian" is also a pejorative for tidy uplift and tacked-on happy endings (the *A.I.* coda, *War of the Worlds*' reunion). Critics say he sands the edges off hard material and reaches for the manipulative emotional button; his instinct for reassurance can blunt genuine darkness.
