# Director Sub-Skill: Denis Villeneuve
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md.*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Awe and dread — the sublime and the sinister in one frame. Humans dwarfed by systems, landscapes, and forces beyond comprehension (aliens, deserts, cartels, prophecy). A near-mystical faith in the *image*: "Frankly, I hate dialogue. Dialogue is for theatre and television… I remember movies because of a strong image."

**Working method.** Storyboards and pre-visualizes heavily; builds meaning through "the movement of the camera, the light, the design… an invisible meaning I call poetry." Strips dialogue to the minimum. Prefers real locations and monumental practical scale, then extends with restrained VFX. Works quietly and closely with actors; a calm, methodical set.

**Signature (spottable).** Vast negative space and tiny human figures against enormous structures; slow deliberate camera moves and glacial pacing; overwhelming low-frequency sound design and brass/vocal drones as physical pressure; muted monochromatic palettes (the ochre *Blade Runner 2049* Vegas, the ash of *Dune*); silhouettes; symmetry used for dread, not whimsy.

**Defining films.** *Incendies* (2010) — the searing breakout; structure as gut-punch · *Prisoners* (2013) — moral-abyss thriller, first Deakins collaboration · *Sicario* (2015) — dread as pure atmosphere; the border-tunnel sequence · *Arrival* (2016) — sci-fi where language and grief bend time · *Blade Runner 2049* (2017) — a sequel that out-scaled the original visually · *Dune: Part One* (2021) & *Part Two* (2024) — image-first epic that still commands the box office.

**Key collaborators.** DPs Roger Deakins (*Prisoners*, *Sicario*, *Blade Runner 2049*) and Greig Fraser (*Dune*). Composers Jóhann Jóhannsson (early), Hans Zimmer (*Blade Runner 2049*, *Dune*). Editor Joe Walker. Actors Timothée Chalamet, Josh Brolin, Rebecca Ferguson, Benicio del Toro.

**The one lesson to steal.** Scale creates emotion. Shrink the human in the frame and the audience feels vulnerability, awe, and dread without a single line of dialogue.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Solemn to a fault — humorless, cold, slow; the seriousness tips into self-importance. Prioritizing image over word leaves some characters underwritten and remote. Detractors call the grandeur ponderous — beautiful but hollow, admiration without warmth.
