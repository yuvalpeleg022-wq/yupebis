# Director Sub-Skill: Federico Fellini
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in Fellini's mode — or to answer "how would Fellini do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Memory, dream, and spectacle as *more* true than realism — the circus/carnival of life; the crisis of the artist and modern man's spiritual emptiness (*La Dolce Vita*, *8½*); Catholicism, sex, provincial nostalgia (Rimini), and the grotesque parade of humanity.

**Working method.** Started in neorealism (co-wrote *Rome, Open City*) then broke into dream-cinema. Cast faces like a painter — open calls to find grotesque, characterful "Fellini faces." Built enormous artificial worlds at Cinecittà Studio 5 rather than shooting locations (even the sea in *And the Ship Sails On* was plastic sheeting). Shot MOS and post-dubbed everything (actors sometimes counted numbers; he directed loudly *during* takes). Drew constant caricatures of every character. Worked from feeling and image, not tight plot.

**Signature (spottable).** The dreamlike procession/parade and the fourth-wall gaze (characters looking into the lens). Baroque, teeming compositions; wide-angle grotesquerie. Post-sync sound design and Nino Rota's circus-melancholy scores. The autobiographical alter-ego (Mastroianni). Fantasy fused seamlessly with memory and present. Studio artificiality embraced, not hidden. "Felliniesque" = carnivalesque excess, clowns, religious kitsch, night.

**Defining films.** *La Strada* (1954) · *Nights of Cabiria* (1957) · *La Dolce Vita* (1960) · *8½* (1963) · *Juliet of the Spirits* (1965) · *Amarcord* (1973); also *Satyricon* (1969), *Roma* (1972).

**Key collaborators.** Composer Nino Rota (nearly every film — inseparable); actress-wife Giulietta Masina (*La Strada*, *Cabiria*); Marcello Mastroianni (alter ego); DPs Otello Martelli and Giuseppe Rotunno; co-writers Tullio Pinelli, Ennio Flaiano, Bernardino Zapponi; designer Danilo Donati; Cinecittà itself.

**The one lesson to steal.** Emotional and psychological truth can outrank literal realism — a built, heightened, dreamlike world can feel *more* real than a documentary one. And casting for the face is casting for the character.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Self-indulgence and shapelessness — the late films (*Casanova*, *City of Women*) sprawl, the parade becomes an end in itself. Accusations of misogyny and of narcissism (endless self-portraits after *8½*). The spectacle can feel hollow; the film-about-director's-block a bit precious.

Sources: *Fellini on Fellini* · Tullio Kezich, *Federico Fellini: His Life and Work* · Deena Boyer, *The Two Hundred Days of 8½*.
