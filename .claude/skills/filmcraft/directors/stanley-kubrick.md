# Director Sub-Skill: Stanley Kubrick
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in Kubrick's mode — or to answer "how would Kubrick do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Human institutions and reason failing against instinct, chance, and the void — man as a violent animal dressed in civilization (*2001*'s bone-to-satellite, *A Clockwork Orange*, *Full Metal Jacket*). Control, dehumanization, the machine (HAL). The gap between rational systems and irrational drives.

**Working method.** Total control; researched obsessively (hundreds of books per project, card-index archives — the unmade *Napoleon* catalogued the man's every day). Demanded huge take counts (*The Shining*'s 100+). Built worlds on soundstages (Vietnam shot in an English gasworks). Technically inventive: the Zeiss f/0.7 NASA lens to shoot *Barry Lyndon* by candlelight; early Steadicam (*The Shining*); front-projection for *2001*. Controlled editing, marketing, even projection.

**Signature (spottable).** The one-point-perspective symmetrical corridor (dead-center vanishing point). Slow ominous zooms and reverse zooms (*Barry Lyndon*). The Steadicam glide (Danny's Big Wheel). The "Kubrick stare" — head lowered, eyes up, into the lens. Classical music against violence (Ligeti, Strauss, Beethoven). Clinical wide-angle distortion. Painterly production design. Cold irony and ambiguity.

**Defining films.** *Dr. Strangelove* (1964) · *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968) · *A Clockwork Orange* (1971) · *Barry Lyndon* (1975) · *The Shining* (1980) · *Full Metal Jacket* (1987) · *Eyes Wide Shut* (1999); early *Paths of Glory* (1957), *The Killing* (1956).

**Key collaborators.** DPs John Alcott (*Barry Lyndon*, *Shining*) and Geoffrey Unsworth (*2001*); effects Douglas Trumbull; co-writers Arthur C. Clarke, Terry Southern, Michael Herr; producer Jan Harlan; actors Peter Sellers, Malcolm McDowell.

**The one lesson to steal.** Research and technical problem-solving are the route to images no one has seen (the candlelit f/0.7 look; the early Steadicam). And control the whole chain — the frame means nothing if projection and context aren't controlled too.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Coldness — the clinical distance drains emotion; characters as specimens. Cruelty on set, especially to actresses (Shelley Duvall's treatment on *The Shining* is a case study in abusive directing). Glacial pacing tips into inertness for some. The perfectionism produced decades-long gaps and unmade projects.

Sources: Michel Ciment, *Kubrick: The Definitive Edition* · Vincent LoBrutto, *Stanley Kubrick: A Biography* · the *Filmworker* doc (Leon Vitali) · Vivian Kubrick's *Making of The Shining*.
