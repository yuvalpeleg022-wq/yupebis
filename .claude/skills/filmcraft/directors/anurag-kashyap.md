# Director Sub-Skill: Anurag Kashyap
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md.*
*Gangs of Wasseypur · Dev.D · Black Friday · Gulaal*

**Core obsession / worldview.** The rot under the Indian dream — power, violence, and the compromised individual. Bollywood's great anti-establishment figure: he takes the sanitized worlds mainstream Hindi cinema avoids (coal-mafia badlands, communal riots, addiction, political rot) and shoots them with unglamorous, profane honesty. Characters are morally soiled and human; ambition curdles into violence; institutions are corrupt by default. He built the template that became **Indian neo-noir** (traceable to his writing on *Satya*, 1998).

**Working method.** Planned scaffolding, then spontaneity. Plans meticulously but "likes being spontaneous" — his DP Rajeev Ravi says "the energy witnessed without planning is the best part of cinema" with him. Casts **unknowns and character actors over stars** so faces carry no baggage; shoots in **real locations** (Mumbai streets, Bihar/UP interiors) instead of sets; lets scenes run raw, improvisational, long. Fuses folk/regional music and diegetic sound with hard cutting. Genuinely willing to break convention — surreal structure (*No Smoking*), five-hour runtime (*Wasseypur*), ugliness without redemption (*Ugly*).

**Signature (spottable).** Real-location grit (grimy lived-in frames, natural light, regional dialect and abuse left in); unknown-face casting; sudden matter-of-fact violence, often blackly funny; folk/rock needle-drops used ironically against carnage (the Wasseypur soundtrack); sprawling chaptered multi-generational structure; dark comedy laced through the bloodshed.

**Defining films.** *Gangs of Wasseypur 1 & 2* (2012) — the sprawling coal-belt crime epic; his defining achievement and a cast-launching machine · *Black Friday* (2004/2007) — docu-realist reconstruction of the 1993 Bombay blasts; release blocked for years · *Dev.D* (2009) — radical modernization of *Devdas* into drugs and neon; his boldest formal reinvention · *Gulaal* (2009) — Rajput politics and rot; his most overtly political, poetry-laced film · *Ugly* (2013) — a bleak kidnap thriller; his most disciplined descent into human awfulness · *No Smoking* (2007) — surrealist neo-noir; a commercial disaster now cult.

**Collaborators.** DP Rajeev Ravi (*Dev.D, Gangs of Wasseypur, Gulaal, Bombay Velvet*). Editor Aarti Bajaj. Composers Amit Trivedi (*Dev.D*) and Sneha Khanwalkar (*Wasseypur*). Actors he made/reused: Nawazuddin Siddiqui, Manoj Bajpayee, Kay Kay Menon, Huma Qureshi, Vineet Kumar Singh. As producer/mentor (Phantom Films) he enabled a whole indie generation.

**The one lesson to steal.** Cast for truth, not wattage, and shoot where it actually smells — unknown faces in real locations buy an authenticity no set or star can fake, and let you point the camera where mainstream cinema won't.

**Weakness / criticism.** No off-switch. His genius is "undeniable, so is his self-indulgence." The freedom that makes him bold makes him baggy — narrative clutter, runtimes that outstay discipline, an inability to restrain itself when it most needs to. His misses (*Bombay Velvet*, *Kennedy*) drown ambition in excess. The nihilism can read as a pose; the violence occasionally becomes the point rather than serving one. He rarely lets himself be cut.
