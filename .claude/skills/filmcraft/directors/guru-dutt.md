# Director Sub-Skill: Guru Dutt
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md.*
*Pyaasa · Kaagaz Ke Phool · Sahib Bibi Aur Ghulam*

Classic Hindi cinema's tragic poet — the artist who made films about the artist crushed by an indifferent, commercialized society. *Pyaasa* (the unrecognized poet celebrated only when presumed dead) and *Kaagaz Ke Phool* (the director destroyed by the studio that once feted him) are melancholy meditations on the suffering of the creative individual, and eerily prefigured his own early death.

**Signature:** built with the great cinematographer V.K. Murthy, his style is **expressionist** — shafts of hard light through deep shadow, low-angle multi-plane compositions, winding camera moves, emotive close-ups. Most famously the ethereal light-beam in "Waqt Ne Kiya," created by bouncing sunlight off mirrors through smoke (in India's first CinemaScope film). His songs are never ornamental; they are the protagonist's inner life, shot with as much care as any dramatic scene.

**Collaborators:** DP V.K. Murthy, composer S.D. Burman, lyricist Sahir Ludhianvi (*Pyaasa*), writer Abrar Alvi, actor-muse Waheeda Rehman.

**The one lesson:** picturize the song as the character's soul made visible — light and camera during a musical number should carry as much meaning as dialogue ever could.

**Weakness:** the melancholy could tip into self-pity and self-mythologizing; pacing is slow and mournful by modern standards; his fusion of personal despair with commercial star-vehicle demands didn't always cohere — *Kaagaz Ke Phool*'s failure so wounded him he never officially directed again.
