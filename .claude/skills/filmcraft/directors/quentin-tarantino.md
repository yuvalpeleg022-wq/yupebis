# Director Sub-Skill: Quentin Tarantino
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md.*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Cinema about cinema — a video-store obsessive who metabolized grindhouse, spaghetti westerns, kung-fu, blaxploitation, and the French New Wave into his own dialect. Recurring: revenge as moral clarity, rewriting history so the oppressed win (Nazis, slavers, and Manson die on *his* terms), and the pleasure of the movies themselves.

**Working method.** Writes long, novelistic scripts by hand, conversations first — lets characters "take over" once the voice is found. Militantly celluloid — shoots and projects film, no digital. Structures in titled chapters, often non-linear. Directs dialogue for rhythm and musicality, letting scenes run far past "necessary" length because the length *is* the suspense.

**Signature (spottable).** The suspense rubber-band — casual talk stretched to breaking, then sudden shocking violence (the *Inglourious Basterds* farmhouse opening; he describes suspense as "a rubber band… stretching it to see how far it can stretch"). Trunk shots; foot fixation; extreme close-ups; profanity-as-music monologues; deep-cut needle-drops; on-screen chapter cards; Mexican standoffs; graphic delayed violence after long calm.

**Defining films.** *Reservoir Dogs* (1992) — heist without the heist; the debut statement · *Pulp Fiction* (1994) — Palme d'Or; the non-linear structure that reshaped '90s indie · *Jackie Brown* (1997) — his most patient, humane, character-first film · *Kill Bill* (2003–04) — pure genre-homage maximalism · *Inglourious Basterds* (2009) — dialogue-as-suspense masterclass and revisionist history · *Django Unchained* (2012) — the western turned on slavery · *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood* (2019) — his elegiac love letter to 1969 LA.

**Key collaborators.** Editor Sally Menke (every film until her death in 2010; Fred Raskin since). Actors Samuel L. Jackson, Uma Thurman, Christoph Waltz, Kurt Russell, Michael Madsen, Tim Roth. DP Robert Richardson (mid-to-late era).

**The one lesson to steal.** Dialogue can *be* the action. A scene of people talking at a table can carry more dread than a shootout if you weaponize the talk and control the tempo.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Indulgence — scenes and films run long because he loves his own writing (*Death Proof* is the cautionary case). Sustained charges over gratuitous violence, the N-word's frequency, his handling of women (plus the Uma Thurman *Kill Bill* stunt-crash and years of Weinstein proximity). The film-reference obsession can read as pastiche standing in for feeling.
