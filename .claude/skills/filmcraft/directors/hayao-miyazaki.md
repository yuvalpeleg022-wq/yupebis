# Director Sub-Skill: Hayao Miyazaki
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md.*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Flight, nature, and pacifism. A Shinto-tinged reverence for the natural world and deep distrust of industrial/military violence — yet never simple: his antagonists are morally grey (Lady Eboshi in *Mononoke* is both destroyer and protector of outcasts). Strong young heroines who act, not wait. Aviation as freedom and as war's tool, held in tension.

**Working method.** No full script — he draws. Sketches, single scenes, "floating emotions," and lets the story grow: "If I know exactly what's going to happen, then there's no point in making the film." Storyboards *are* the screenplay, often drawn while production already runs. Draws and retouches key frames by hand himself (~80,000 for *Mononoke*). Militant defender of hand-drawn 2D over CG.

**Signature (spottable).** "Ma" — deliberate stillness, "the time in between," where characters sit, sigh, or watch a stream, not to advance plot but to let tension breathe into a wider dimension. Ecstatic flight sequences; food animated with loving detail; wind and weather as living presences; kind dignified elders; villains who turn out to be human.

**Defining films.** *Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind* (1984) — the eco-pacifist template · *Castle in the Sky* (1986) — adventure and the ruin of technological hubris · *My Neighbor Totoro* (1988) — the "ma" masterpiece; childhood and nature, almost plotless by design · *Kiki's Delivery Service* (1989) — gentle coming-of-age · *Princess Mononoke* (1997) — his grandest moral-grey ecological epic · *Spirited Away* (2001) — Oscar winner; the imaginative peak · *The Wind Rises* (2013) — beauty and complicity; an aircraft designer's dream and its wartime cost.

**Key collaborators.** Composer Joe Hisaishi (nearly every film). Co-founder/producer Toshio Suzuki and director Isao Takahata at Studio Ghibli. The Ghibli animation team he trained.

**The one lesson to steal.** Give the film room to breathe. Build in stillness — moments where nothing "happens" — so the emotional and natural world can register. Pacing is not just plot velocity.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** A punishing, autocratic perfectionist (documented in *The Kingdom of Dreams and Madness*) — brutal on his animators, famously harsh even about his son Gorō's work. Plots turn loose or muddy in the back half (*Howl's Moving Castle*, *The Wind Rises*) precisely because he improvises structure. Repeats his own motifs (flight, plucky girl, eco-collapse) across a career.
