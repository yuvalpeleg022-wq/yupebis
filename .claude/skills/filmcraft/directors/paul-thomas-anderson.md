# Director Sub-Skill: Paul Thomas Anderson
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md. Channel this to write, shoot, or critique in PTA's mode — or to answer "how would Paul Thomas Anderson do X."*

**Core obsession / worldview.** Substitute families and broken/absent fathers; American ambition, capitalism, and the hunger for connection or transcendence; charismatic controlling men and the people who orbit them (*There Will Be Blood*, *The Master*, *Boogie Nights*, *Phantom Thread*). Loneliness beneath spectacle.

**Working method.** Evolved from a maximalist (Altman/Scorsese-influenced ensemble virtuosity, roaming Steadicam) to a stiller, classical style. Deeply cinephilic; writes his own scripts, usually built around a magnetic performance. Shoots on film (a vocal celluloid champion) and increasingly operates the camera himself. Long patient takes and controlled staging; researches period detail obsessively (the oil industry for *There Will Be Blood*, the fashion house for *Phantom Thread*). Builds around music and rhythm.

**Signature (spottable).** The roaming Steadicam long take (the *Boogie Nights* pool-party opening) — then, later, locked, symmetrical compositions and slow zooms (*The Master*, *There Will Be Blood*). Coincidence and ensemble convergence (*Magnolia*'s frogs). Faces held long; explosive performance peaks ("I drink your milkshake"). Jonny Greenwood's dissonant string scores. Period Americana; natural light and 65/70mm grandeur. Volatile male duets.

**Defining films.** *Boogie Nights* (1997) · *Magnolia* (1999) · *Punch-Drunk Love* (2002) · *There Will Be Blood* (2007) · *The Master* (2012) · *Phantom Thread* (2017) · *Licorice Pizza* (2021).

**Key collaborators.** Composer Jonny Greenwood (from *There Will Be Blood*); DPs Robert Elswit (most films) and Mihai Mălaimare Jr. (*The Master*, 65mm); actors Philip Seymour Hoffman (muse until his death), Daniel Day-Lewis, Philip Baker Hall, Julianne Moore; producer JoAnne Sellar.

**The one lesson to steal.** Build the film around a performance and a rhythm, and let camera style serve the character's psychology — knowing when to move (early) and when to hold dead still (later) is a maturation, not a rulebook. Research the world until you can improvise inside it truthfully.

**Weakness / honest criticism.** Self-indulgence and structural sprawl, especially early (*Magnolia*'s runtime and frogs divide viewers as sublime or precious). The influences can show as pastiche (Altman, Scorsese, Kubrick). *The Master* is deliberately elusive to the point of frustration; the male-genius fixation recurs, prizing enigma over resolution.

Sources: PTA interviews in *Film Comment*, *Sight & Sound*, KCRW's *The Treatment* · the *There Will Be Blood* and *Phantom Thread* press cycles · *American Cinematographer* on *The Master*'s 65mm.
