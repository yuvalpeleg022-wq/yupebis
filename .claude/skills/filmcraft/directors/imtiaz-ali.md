# Director Sub-Skill: Imtiaz Ali
*Load with the base `filmcraft` SKILL.md.*
*Jab We Met · Rockstar · Tamasha · Highway · Love Aaj Kal*

**Core obsession / worldview.** He makes one film repeatedly, and admits it: a person asleep inside a "correct" life, woken by love, travel, or a stranger. His real subject isn't romance; it's self-recognition — love is the accident that forces the protagonist to stop performing the assigned identity and find the "real" self underneath. Travel is load-bearing: he says you can't understand filmmaking without travelling, and nearly every film routes self-discovery through a physical journey (Manali, Corsica, Kashmir-to-nowhere, Delhi-to-Kolkata).

**Working method.** Character-first, plot-loose. Builds a restless, often broken protagonist and lets geography and songs carry the arc rather than a tight three-act engine. Music is the narrative's load-bearing wall — in *Rockstar* the hero can only reach his own feeling *through* song, so the soundtrack is the script's interior monologue. Works with composers who improvise "sans any safety net" (his words on A.R. Rahman); lets scenes run on mood and performance; folds actual travel into the shoot.

**Signature (spottable).** Non-linear love story (flashback/present intercut — *Love Aaj Kal*'s two eras, *Rockstar*'s frame narrative); the song as scene, not break (interior change carried by "Nadaan Parindey," "Agar Tum Saath Ho"); the journey as structure; the late "waking up" monologue where the trapped protagonist names their suppressed self (Ved in *Tamasha*); warm sun-flared handheld intimacy; the restless talkative female foil against a repressed male lead.

**Defining films.** *Jab We Met* (2007) — the template as pure crowd-pleasing joy · *Rockstar* (2011) — his most ambitious music-as-narrative fusion; Rahman's finest Bollywood score does the storytelling · *Tamasha* (2015) — the thesis stated outright: the cost of an inauthentic life; divisive but most personal · *Highway* (2014) — his best female arc; a kidnapping becomes liberation · *Love Aaj Kal* (2009) — the dual-timeline move that became his signature · *Jab Harry Met Sejal* (2017) — a late, weaker restatement; the formula fraying.

**Collaborators.** Composers A.R. Rahman (*Rockstar, Highway, Tamasha*) and Pritam (*Jab We Met, Love Aaj Kal*); lyricist Irshad Kamil (near-constant, central — his words are half the storytelling); DPs Anil Mehta, Ravi Varman; muse Ranbir Kapoor (*Rockstar, Tamasha*), Deepika Padukone, Kareena Kapoor.

**The one lesson to steal.** Build the emotional arc into the soundtrack so music does what dialogue can't, then trust the audience to feel the change instead of explaining it.

**Weakness / criticism.** The manic-pixie problem and the same-film problem. Female leads are frequently plot-devices for male self-actualization (*Tamasha*'s Tara has no family or interiority of her own). Called out for making one film on loop and for regressive handling of female sexuality even in otherwise-praised films (*Highway*). Plotting goes shapeless when music and star chemistry don't cover for it (*Jab Harry Met Sejal*).
