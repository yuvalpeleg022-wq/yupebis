## 0. How to read this dossier

### 0.1 Research method and honest limits

All research for this pass was done through live web search during this session, dated 2026-11-06. Two limits shape how much weight each claim can carry:

1. **The network blocked direct page fetches from Wikipedia**, and full-text pages could not be opened. Claims come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading every page end to end. Each fact below is attributed to the source the summary drew on.
2. **Most of the detailed TMNT plot material online lives on fan wikis** (Turtlepedia, mutantooze, TV Tropes). These are usually accurate on broad strokes and less reliable on issue numbers and fine detail. They are labelled as such.

No citation in this document was invented. Where I hold a belief from general knowledge that **this pass could not confirm**, it is marked **[UNVERIFIED]** and must not be treated as canon until checked against the printed issue or an official collection.

### 0.2 Source-confidence tiers

| Tier | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **[A]** | Publisher, licensor or creator-run page; direct interview with the filmmaker | miragelicensing.com, teenagemutantninjaturtles.com, Post Magazine / Deadline interviews |
| **[B]** | Professional journalism, criticism, trade press, established review sites | Slashfilm, Empire, Den of Geek, CBR, Retcon Punch, fxguide |
| **[C]** | Fan wiki / fan-edited database / blog | Turtlepedia, TV Tropes, Comic Vine, mutantooze, personal review blogs |

### 0.3 Canon labels used across all project documents

- **CANON-M**: established in Mirage Studios comics (1984 onward).
- **CANON-I**: established in IDW Publishing comics (2011–2024 run).
- **CANON-O**: established in another continuity (cartoons, films). Recorded for awareness only and not used unless stated.
- **ORIGINAL**: our creative decision for this film's continuity.

## 1. TMNT SOURCE MATERIAL

### 1.1 Mirage Studios: the original comics (1984 onward)

**Publication origin.** Kevin Eastman and Peter Laird founded Mirage Studios in March 1984 in their own home and conceived the book as a parody of popular superhero-comic tropes. The first print run of about 3,000 black-and-white copies was funded by Eastman's $500 tax return, $200 of Laird's savings and $1,300 borrowed from Eastman's uncle. Issue #1 carries a May 1984 cover date and was a surprise success. [B] ([Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/30862/complete-history-teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles))

**Issue #1 (May 1984).** First appearance of the Turtles, Splinter, the Shredder and the Foot Clan, with the Turtles' origin. [B] ([Grand Comics Database #1](https://www.comics.org/issue/245469/))

The Mirage backstory, as the search summaries report it [C/B] ([Wikipedia: Splinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splinter_(Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles)); [Turtlepedia: Origin](https://turtlepedia.fandom.com/wiki/Origin)):
- **Hamato Yoshi**, a high-ranking member of the Foot Clan in Japan, became caught in a deadly dispute with fellow ninja **Oroku Nagi** over a woman, **Tang Shen**. Yoshi killed Nagi.
- Dishonoured, **Yoshi and Shen fled to New York**.
- Nagi's younger brother **Oroku Saki** was taken in by the clan, trained as a ninja, and later **murdered Yoshi in New York** to avenge his brother.
- **Splinter**, Yoshi's pet rat, survived. He took refuge in the sewers, where four baby turtles had fallen after being dropped by a young boy. Exposure to a mutagen mutated all five.
- In Mirage, **Splinter and Yoshi are different beings**: the rat is not the man.
- [UNVERIFIED] Splinter learned ninjutsu by watching and mimicking Yoshi from his cage, and Tang Shen died at Saki's hands. Both are widely repeated; confirm against #1.

**Shredder's first death (#1).** The Turtles challenge the Shredder to a rooftop fight to the death. **Donatello knocks him from the roof and he explodes in mid-air.** Commentators describe the death as abrupt and unceremonious, because the villain dies in his debut issue. [C/B] ([TV Tropes: TMNT Mirage](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/ComicBook/TeenageMutantNinjaTurtlesMirage); [Scifi Continuums re-read](https://scificontinnum.substack.com/p/tmnt-re-read-mirage-issue-3-the-great))

**Early cosmic stories (#4–#7, 1985–86).** The Utroms (TCRI aliens in robot bodies), Professor Honeycutt / the Fugitoid and the **Triceratons** enter through a teleport-accident storyline that carries the Turtles off-world. [A] ([Mirage Group: Vol. 1 #4](https://miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/04/04.html), [#5](https://miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/05/05.html), [#6](https://miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/06/06.html), [#7](https://miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/07/07.html); [B] [Reviews From My Couch: #5–7](https://reviewsfrommycouch.com/?p=3553))

**The micro-series one-shots (1985–86).** Each Turtle received a solo one-shot. These are the cleanest Mirage-era character studies:
- *Raphael #1, "Me, Myself and I"* (1985). Raph loses control while sparring and goes after Michelangelo with a wrench. Out alone, he meets **Casey Jones**, who arrives as an opponent. This is Casey's first appearance. [A/B/C] ([Mirage Group: Raphael](https://miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/raph/raphael.html); [Major Spoilers retro review](https://majorspoilers.com/2023/02/12/retro-review-raphael-teenage-mutant-ninja-turtle-1-may-1985); [Wikipedia: Casey Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_Jones_(Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles)))
- *Michaelangelo #1* (1985). A Christmas story: Mikey takes in a stray cat, **Klunk**, and stops thugs who hijacked a truck of toys meant for an orphanage. One reviewer reads it as the only one-shot that clearly fleshes out its lead. [A/B] ([Mirage Group: Michaelangelo](https://miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/mike/michaelangelo.html); [ComicsAlliance](https://www.comicsalliance.com/bizarro-back-issues-tmnt-michelangelo-saves-christmas-1985/); [Reviews From My Couch](https://reviewsfrommycouch.com/?p=3549))
- *Donatello #1* (c. August 1986) and *Leonardo #1, "What Goes Around… Comes Around!"* (December 1986). In Leonardo's issue he fights the Foot alone across New York rooftops on Christmas Eve while April and Splinter prepare a holiday meal, and the story leads into the Foot's return. [C] ([Turtlepedia](https://turtlepedia.fandom.com/wiki/What_Goes_Around..._Comes_Around!); [mycomicscollection](https://mycomicscollection.com/en/blog/leonardo-comics-story/))

**Shredder's resurrection.** The Foot used a colony of worms that consume remains and take the form of what they eat. Three earlier experiments produced malformed **Shredder Elite / clones**; on a later attempt the worms fed on Saki's own remains and a new Shredder rose. [C] ([Turtlepedia: Shredder clones](https://turtlepedia.fandom.com/wiki/Shredder_clones_(Mirage)); [TV Tropes: Mirage Foot Clan](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/TeenageMutantNinjaTurtlesMirageTheFootClan))

**Exile.** The Shredder and the Foot ambush Leonardo, beat him nearly to death and throw him through the window of April's shop. They burn the building, and with Casey's help the family escapes to a farmhouse belonging to Casey's grandmother in **Northampton, Massachusetts**. [C] ([Shredder (Mirage), fan wiki](https://antagonists.fandom.com/wiki/Shredder_(Mirage)); [Turtlepedia: "Silent Partner" summary](https://turtlepedia.fandom.com/wiki/Silent_Partner?oldid=4783)) The exact issue sequence varies between sources.

### 1.2 "Return to New York" (Mirage Vol. 1 #19–21, 1989)

**This is the most important single source for our film's tone.**

- **Creators:** Eastman and Laird, with Jim Lawson pencilling Book Three. [A] ([Mirage Group #19](https://miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/19/19.html), [#21](https://miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/21/21.html))
- **Premise:** After healing in exile, the Turtles return to New York to confront the resurrected Shredder. [B] ([Doom Rocket](https://doomrocket.substack.com/p/mutant-turtles-kick-ninja-asses-in); [Reviews From My Couch](https://reviewsfrommycouch.com/?p=3543))
- **Internal conflict:** The arc carries the tension between **Leonardo and Raphael**. [B] ([Reviews From My Couch](https://reviewsfrommycouch.com/?p=3543))
- **Climax:** In #21 the Turtles split inside the Foot stronghold. Donatello and Michelangelo hold off two Shredder Elite, Raphael pushes deeper and is ambushed by four experienced ninja, and **Leonardo faces the true Shredder in the heart of the stronghold**. [A/C] ([Mirage Group #21](https://www.miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/21/21.html); [Turtlepedia: RTNY Book Three](https://turtlepedia.fandom.com/wiki/Return_to_New_York,_book_three))
- **Outcome:** **Leonardo kills Oroku Saki**, for the second time in the series and, by most readings, permanently. [B] ([Doom Rocket](https://doomrocket.substack.com/p/mutant-turtles-kick-ninja-asses-in))
- [UNVERIFIED] Leonardo decapitates Shredder and the body is burned so it cannot return. This is often repeated and not confirmed in this pass. The confirmed setting is **inside the stronghold, not a rooftop**.

**Why it matters to us:** RTNY is the template for the film's spine. Defeat, physical and psychological breakage, exile, recovery as a family, and a return to the city to take back what was lost. Its four-way split climax, with each brother facing his own wall, is a structural model for converging arcs. **Our story will not adapt RTNY.** We take the *shape*: breakage → exile → return. The plot will be different.

### 1.3 Post-Shredder Foot politics: "City at War" (Mirage Vol. 1, c. #50–62, 1992–93)

- After Saki's death the New York Foot **fractured into warring factions**. [B/C] ([bamsmackpow on the IDW homage](https://bamsmackpow.com/2019/05/06/city-at-war-teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-no-93); [The Nostalgia Spot: Ultimate Collection Vol. 5](https://thenostalgiaspot.com/2020/02/26/teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-the-ultimate-collection-vol-5/))
- **Karai**, a leader of the Foot in Japan sent by the clan's governing council, comes to New York to restore order. She captures Leonardo and offers a truce: destroy the Shredder Elite, who remain honour-bound to avenge their master, and the Foot will stand down. [C] ([Wikipedia: Karai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karai_(Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles)); [Mirage Group #59](https://miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/59/59.html), [#61](https://miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/61/61.html))
- Fan sources say the loyalists killed Karai's daughter during the conflict, and that Leonardo killed the last Elite, sealing the truce. [C] ([Wikipedia: Foot Clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_Clan))
- **Discrepancy:** sources disagree on whether the arc starts at #50 or #56 and on Karai's first issue (#53?). Check the Ultimate Collection checklist before citing a number.

**Why it matters to us:** Mirage already presents the Foot as **a hierarchical institution that outlives its leader**, with a council in Japan, an American branch, an elite guard bound by oath, and succession politics. That is the seed of a Foot Clan that feels like a real organisation.

### 1.4 IDW Publishing (2011–2024 main run)

**Launch and creators.** The 2011 relaunch was co-plotted by co-creator **Kevin Eastman** and **Tom Waltz**, with **Bobby Curnow** as editor. [B] ([Wikipedia summary: TMNT (IDW)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles_(IDW_Publishing)); [bamsmackpow](https://bamsmackpow.com/2019/03/19/tmnt-shredder-in-hell-no-2-review-saki/))

**The reincarnation origin.** In IDW, **Splinter is Hamato Yoshi**, a reincarnated warrior of feudal Japan, and **the four Turtles are reincarnations of Yoshi's four sons**. In the past life Yoshi and **Oroku Saki** were both of the Foot. They were comrades and in some accounts close friends, until Yoshi's break with the clan. Foot ninja attacked his home and killed **Tang Shen**. Yoshi killed the attackers before they reached the children, but the family was later murdered. Accounts differ on whether Saki killed Shen directly; one summary describes a fire. [B/C] ([Looper: Untold Truth of Splinter](https://looper.com/1354906/untold-truth-master-splinter-ninja-turtles); [Wikipedia: Splinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splinter_(Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles)); [Comic Vine: Hamato Yoshi](https://comicvine.gamespot.com/hamato-yoshi/4005-55904/))
- At least one commentator calls the reincarnation choice **controversial**. [B] ([veve.me origin feature](https://www.veve.me/blog/fandoms/7-key-moments-that-shaped-the-tmnt-origin-mythos/))
- IDW also gives the Foot an ancient founding myth: the ronin **Takeshi Tatsuo**, betrayed by his master, founds the clan and is later reincarnated as Oroku Saki. [C] ([Wikipedia: Foot Clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_Clan))

**Raphael in IDW #1 (2011).** In the modern-day origin **Raphael was separated from his family** after the mutation and spent about fifteen months alone as a near-feral street vagrant, unsure of his own name. He meets **Casey Jones** when he steps in as Casey's drunk, abusive father beats him. The two become street vigilantes before Raph is reunited with his brothers. **Old Hob**, a one-eyed mutant cat, hates Splinter, who took the eye protecting the infant turtles. [B/C] ([Big Shiny Robot review](https://bigshinyrobot.com/56113/review-teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-idw-1/); [Comic Vine: Raphael](https://comicvine.gamespot.com/raphael/4005-37764/); [TV Tropes: TMNT IDW](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/ComicBook/TeenageMutantNinjaTurtlesIDW))

**"City Fall" (#21–28, 2013).** The fox-sorceress **Kitsune** uses dark magic to rewrite **Leonardo's memories** so that he sees Splinter as the enemy and the Shredder as his master. Shredder forbids Leonardo lethal force in case killing snaps the conditioning. When Splinter attacks Shredder, Leonardo steps out in Foot regalia to defend him. Issue #25 opens a full-scale war on the city. Leo is later recovered, but the scars run deep. [B/C] ([Den of Geek: Guide to City Fall](https://www.denofgeek.com/comics/teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-a-guide-to-city-fall/); [Turtlepedia: City Fall](https://turtlepedia.fandom.com/wiki/City_Fall); [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/exclusive-idws-teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-prepare-for-cityfall/))

**Death of Donatello (#44, March 2015).** While his brothers attack Krang, Donatello is ambushed at home by Bebop and Rocksteady and struck down with a sledgehammer. Later issues bring him back (his mind survives outside his body), but #44 was reported as a death. [B] ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/holy-cow-the-latest-ninja-turtles-comic-has-one-hell-o-1692457941); [Cosmic Book News](https://cosmicbook.news/spoiler-death-teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-44); [Supermarcey review](https://supermarcey.com/2015/04/10/comic-review-teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-44))

**Issue #50 (October 2015): Splinter kills Shredder and inherits the Foot.** The Turtles beat Shredder's lieutenants and Karai in a ritual gauntlet, winning Splinter single combat. **Splinter defeats and kills his former friend and becomes leader of the Foot Clan.** Karai returns to Japan and leaves the Foot in his hands. [A/B] ([teenagemutantninjaturtles.com](https://www.teenagemutantninjaturtles.com/?p=10462); [Retcon Punch #50](https://retcon-punch.com/2015/10/08/teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-50); [ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/comics/news/teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-new-female-turtle-jennika))
- **IDW "City at War" (#93–100, 2019)** returns to this. Karai tries to take the Foot back by force from Splinter. [B] ([bamsmackpow](https://bamsmackpow.com/2019/05/06/city-at-war-teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-no-93))
- *Shredder in Hell* follows Saki's soul after death. [B] ([bamsmackpow](https://bamsmackpow.com/2019/03/19/tmnt-shredder-in-hell-no-2-review-saki/))

**Michelangelo in IDW.** Reviewers repeatedly name **loneliness** as a theme for Mikey, including his isolation without a team and his loneliness as a celebrity. In "Order from Chaos" he joins Old Hob's Mutanimals, trusts them, uncovers Hob's deception, and is cut and blamed by Hob for breaking the group apart. [B] ([Retcon Punch #53](https://retcon-punch.com/2015/12/30/teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-53/); [Flickering Myth](https://www.flickeringmyth.com/?p=165507))

### 1.6 The Triceratons

- **Oldest Mirage creation.** Fan sources say Laird sketched Triceratons before the Turtles existed. Their first published appearance was in Mirage's *Fugitoid* one-shot (1985). [C] ([CBR: Triceraton Empire](https://www.cbr.com/tmnt-ninja-turtles-triceraton-empire-dinosaurs/); [Mirage Group: Fugitoid](https://miragelicensing.com/comics/mirage/volume01/fugitoid/fugitoid.html))
- **Mirage society.** A militaristic **Triceraton Republic** with a senate, at war with a human Federation. Most versions say their homeworld was destroyed and they now live on ship-sized asteroid vessels. [B/C] ([CBR](https://www.cbr.com/tmnt-ninja-turtles-triceraton-empire-dinosaurs/); [official TMNT Showcase: Triceratons](https://www.teenagemutantninjaturtles.com/blog/tmnt-showcase-triceratons/)) **Caveat:** the "Federation war" framing could not be confirmed for Mirage specifically; one search tied it to the 2003 cartoon. [UNVERIFIED for Mirage]
- **Zanramon** (fan sources) turned the Republic into a dictatorship and launched conquests; after a failed invasion of Earth he was deposed, and the Triceratons came to respect the Turtles. [C] Which continuity this applies to is unconfirmed.
- **IDW origin.** The Utroms abducted prehistoric triceratops from Earth and genetically engineered them into a **warrior slave race**. **Zog** led a rebellion that ended Emperor Quanin's reign. [B] ([bamsmackpow: TMNT Universe #16](https://bamsmackpow.com/2017/11/29/teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-universe-16-review); [Retcon Punch tag: Zog](https://retcon-punch.com/tag/zog); [Turtlepedia: From the Heart, For the Herd](https://turtlepedia.fandom.com/wiki/From_the_Heart,_For_the_Herd,_part_1))
- **2012 cartoon:** Triceratons from Dimension X. [C, CANON-O, not used]

**Implication for the post-credit scene.** Every continuity agrees on one thing: the Triceratons are **a disciplined, technologically advanced military civilisation shaped by war and by a lost or stolen homeworld**. The post-credit staging (formation, chain of command, clipped reports) fits all versions. **ORIGINAL decision:** we do not commit to the Mirage *or* IDW origin on screen. The scene shows a military bureaucracy, not its history.

## 2. CHARACTER RESEARCH

Each profile gives **(a)** what the cited canon supports, **(b)** what changes between continuities, **(c)** implications for our film. Full arcs and psychology go in `02_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`.

### 2.1 Oroku Saki / The Shredder
- **Mirage:** a Foot-trained avenger who kills Yoshi over his brother Nagi's death. He uses the Foot as a criminal army in New York. He dies in #1, returns via the worms, breaks Leonardo, drives the family into exile, and is killed by Leonardo in #21. (§1.1–1.2)
- **IDW:** a man bound across lifetimes to Yoshi. He was once Yoshi's comrade or friend. Strategist and emperor of a modern criminal empire, he uses manipulation (City Fall) as a weapon as much as blades. He dies at Splinter's hand in #50. (§1.4)
- **Constant across versions:** vengeance as identity; the Foot as an instrument of his will; a **personal** link to Yoshi; being **physically dangerous in single combat**; and **psychological warfare** (breaking Leo by body in Mirage, by mind in IDW).
- **Implications:** for a frightening, strategic villain, our Shredder should win the middle of the film **through information and leverage, not only force**. Mirage contributes the brutality; IDW contributes the personal history and the turning of one brother against the family.

### 2.2 Hamato Yoshi & Splinter
- **Mirage:** Yoshi and Splinter are **separate** beings. The rat is the student of a dead man and the keeper of his grievance. (§1.1)
- **IDW:** Splinter **is** Yoshi reborn: a father carrying a past life, whose final act is to kill his old friend and take over the clan he fled. (§1.4)
- **Implications:** both versions make Splinter a **father who inherited a war**. The richer drama is in the gap between *the honour he teaches* and *the vengeance he may still want*. IDW #50, where Splinter becomes the Foot's master, shows how close the two sides are.

### 2.3 Leonardo
- **Mirage:** he faces the Foot alone on rooftops (Leonardo #1). He is the brother Shredder nearly kills, and the one who goes into the stronghold and ends Saki (RTNY). (§1.1–1.2)
- **IDW:** City Fall turns the leader into the enemy's soldier and leaves deep scars. (§1.4)
- **Implications:** his arc in both canons is **leadership tested by personal failure**. The brief's "fear of failure" is fully supported.

### 2.4 Raphael
- **Mirage:** temper turned on his own brother (the wrench); alone in the city; meets Casey as an adversary. Friction with Leonardo is central in RTNY. (§1.1–1.2)
- **IDW:** separated from the family, feral and nameless, finds family in Casey. (§1.4)
- **Implications:** his anger is **the anger of the one left behind**. Canon gives him a lonely street life and a surrogate brother. Our "revenge vs. self-control" arc can be grounded in that.

### 2.5 Donatello
- **Mirage:** the Turtle who kills the Shredder the first time (#1). His one-shot exists (1986), but its content was not verified in this pass. (§1.1)
- **IDW:** the family's technologist; struck down at home while alone, which was publicised as a death (#44). (§1.4)
- **Implications:** canon links Donatello to **technology and to being the one isolated when danger arrives**. The brief's "secrecy and dangerous discoveries" is **largely ORIGINAL** layered on that base, and I will note it as such.

### 2.6 Michelangelo
- **Mirage:** the Christmas one-shot is the warmest character study in the line. He goes out alone, connects with strangers, adopts Klunk, and protects children's gifts. (§1.1)
- **IDW:** loneliness is a recurring reviewer-identified theme. He seeks belonging outside the family (the Mutanimals) and is betrayed. (§1.4)
- **Implications:** the brief's "humour, loneliness and emotional strength" matches canon closely. **Emotional strength** is the key: in both canons Mikey is the one who *reaches outward*.

### 2.7 Supporting cast, for awareness
- **Casey Jones**: Mirage street vigilante (Raphael #1); IDW abused teenager turned vigilante. (§1.1, §1.4)
- **April O'Neil**: in Mirage, a computer programmer and assistant to Baxter Stockman. [C] ([Wikipedia: April O'Neil](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/April_O%27Neil))
- **Karai**: Foot leader from Japan; ally of necessity; successor figure. (§1.3)

**Open decision for Phase 2:** whether April and Casey appear at all. A crowded cast thins the four arcs. My early instinct is **one** human ally with weight, not both as cameos.

## 3. DIRECTOR RESEARCH: THE RUSSO BROTHERS

The brief asks for their principles as **high-level inspiration**, with an original visual identity. Findings below are drawn from interviews; the lessons are mine.

### 3.1 Genre grounding: the '70s thriller as chassis
- They framed *Winter Soldier* as a **1970s-style political thriller** and named *The French Connection*, *Three Days of the Condor* and *The Conversation* as formative influences. [B] ([Empire](https://www.empireonline.com/movies/news/joe-anthony-russo-captain-america-winter-solider/); [Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/russo-brothers-interview-cap-2); [JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/the-russo-brothers-talk-politics-stealth-suit-and-more-for-captain-america-the-winter-soldier/))
- Joe Russo: a thriller needs **grounded, high stakes** with characters in real danger. [B] ([Slashfilm interview](https://www.slashfilm.com/interview-captain-america-the-winter-soldier-directors-promise-a-significant-shift-in-the-marvel-universe))
- DP Trent Opaloch says the team referenced *Marathon Man* and *Three Days of the Condor* in prep. [B] ([Matthew Toffolo interview](https://matthewtoffolo.com/2016/04/10/interview-with-cinematographer-trent-opaloch-captain-america-civil-war))
- **Lesson:** choose a real-world genre chassis first, and keep the fantastic elements behind it until the story has earned them. For us that chassis is the **organised-crime procedural / revenge thriller**.

### 3.2 Action as character
- Anthony Russo: "We use action very specifically to find ways to express character and express narrative through action." [B] ([SAHM Reviews interview](http://www.sahmreviews.com/2016/05/captain-america-civil-war-russo-brothers-interview.html); [Deadline Q&A](https://deadline.com/2016/05/captain-america-civil-war-joe-anthony-russo-chris-evans-robert-downey-jr-marvel-1201750033/))
- For *Civil War* they looked for **each hero's vulnerability** and played those vulnerabilities against each other, aiming not to take a side. [B] ([SAHM Reviews](http://www.sahmreviews.com/2016/05/captain-america-civil-war-russo-brothers-interview.html))
- **Lesson:** every fight must change a relationship or reveal a weakness. A fight in which nothing changes is cut.

### 3.3 Handheld vérité and a grounded fighting style
- Anthony Russo: *Winter Soldier* was shot handheld in a loose vérité style, and the approach carried into the action. [B] ([Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/interview-captain-america-the-winter-soldier-directors-promise-a-significant-shift-in-the-marvel-universe); [HitFix/Uproxx set visit](https://uproxx.com/hitfix/set-visit-the-russo-brothers-bring-gritty-action-to-captain-america-2))
- The fighting was described as "ground and pound" close combat rather than high-flying, and Joe Russo told GQ the fight style would make the film distinctive. [B] ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-russo-brothers-put-captain-america-in-a-cage-match-1833895352); [Uproxx: elevator fight](https://uproxx.com/movies/captain-america-winter-soldier-mcu-russo-brothers))
- The elevator fight was the first scene shot. [B] ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-russo-brothers-put-captain-america-in-a-cage-match-1833895352); [Looper](https://www.looper.com/771462/chris-evans-confirms-what-we-suspected-about-the-russo-brothers-on-set-behavior))
- **Lesson and our divergence:** handheld conveys a combatant's subjectivity. Unmotivated shake destroys geography. **ORIGINAL rule:** our camera is *locked or slow* when a Turtle is in control and goes *handheld only when control is lost*, so camera grammar tracks emotional state.

### 3.4 Preparation: boards → previs → iteration
- The Russos describe a loop of **storyboards → previs → repeated rebuilding** of a sequence until they know exactly how to shoot it. Editors **Jeffrey Ford and Matthew Schmidt** are named as close collaborators. [A] ([Post Magazine, June 2016](https://digital.copcomm.com/i/691665-june-2016/23))
- The *Civil War* airport fight was planned over weeks at a table **with action figures** to test line-ups, then previs. [B] ([Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/captain-america-civil-war-airport-fight-scene-planning/))
- Joe Russo called the film "brutalist" in tone and chose a concrete tarmac as the setting to match. [B] ([Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/captain-america-civil-war-airport-fight-scene-planning/))
- **Lesson:** the location *is* the tone statement, and set pieces are designed as maps before they are designed as shots. Every action scene in `07_CINEMATOGRAPHY_AND_SHOT_LIST.md` will open with a top-down geography map.

### 3.5 Structure: villain-driven plotting and converging lines
- *Infinity War* was told largely from **Thanos's point of view**. The Russos described the film as **a heist with Thanos running it** while the heroes race for the MacGuffins. [B] ([Motion Picture Association](https://www.motionpictures.org/2018/04/the-russo-brothers-say-avengers-infinity-war-is-a-thanos-movie/); [CBR interview](https://www.cbr.com/interview-russo-brothers-infinity-wars-thanos-spider-man-wolverine/); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/the-avengers-infinity-war-directors-reveal-which-two-characters-are-getting-the-most-screen-time))
- They said the film could have become Thor's story if he had landed the killing blow; because Thanos won, it became his. [B] (same sources)
- **Lesson:** the antagonist drives the plot and the heroes drive the theme. Our Shredder needs **a plan with stages** that the audience can track, so each Turtle storyline can be cut against a clock the villain controls.

### 3.6 Camera and format (for awareness, not imitation)
- *Civil War*: ARRI Alexa XT with Panavision anamorphics as the main package; Alexa 65 for the airport and other sequences on Technocrane, Steadicam, dolly and drone. [B] ([Codex case study](https://codex.online/casestudies/heroic-measures-for-captain-america-civil-war); [fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=185876); [Studio Daily](https://studiodaily.com/2016/04/studiodaily-dossier-10-things-we-know-about-marvels-civil-war))
- **Lesson:** large-format capture was reserved for the scale moments. Our format decisions follow the same logic (see §4).

### 3.7 What we deliberately do NOT take
- MCU-style quip rhythm undercutting tension. Our humour belongs to Michelangelo and must come from character under pressure.
- Brightly lit, evenly exposed daytime action. We are a night film.
- Franchise-connective tissue in the main body. The brief is explicit: the saga lives only in the post-credit scene.

## 4. EARLY VISUAL-LANGUAGE CONCLUSIONS (input to Phase 3)

These are proposals, not locks.

1. **Two visual worlds.** The **Lair** is warm, handmade, tungsten and candle, patina and repair, and shot on longer takes. The **Foot** is cold, institutional, sodium-vapour and fluorescent, symmetrical, hard architecture.
2. **Restrained green and red.** Green is not a neon glow. It is **mutagen and life**: wet moss, oxidised copper, the turtles' skin under work lights. Red is **Foot authority**: lacquer, tail-lights, emergency lighting, never ambient.
3. **Camera as emotional state** (§3.3): locked or slow when in control, handheld when control is lost.
4. **Geography first** (§3.4): every set piece has a readable map, a stated objective and a visible clock.
5. **Format:** spherical anamorphic-look 2.39:1 for the body of the film. [Proposal] Large-format capture reserved for the rooftops and the climax so the scale lands when it matters.
6. **Practical bodies.** Puppetry/performance-capture hybrid with practical props and wet surfaces, so the turtles sit in the same light as the sets.

## 6. DIRECTOR'S CHALLENGES TO THE BRIEF (for discussion before Phase 2)

I am raising these now so they are decided before story work, not after:

1. **The "science fiction" genre tag vs. no multiverse and no aliens.** If Krang and the Utroms are off the table, the science fiction has to come from the **mutagen itself**. I recommend the mutagen as a real, coveted substance inside the plot: something the Foot wants to weaponise and Donatello discovers more about than he should. That serves Donatello's "dangerous discoveries" arc and keeps the genre honest without breaking the grounded tone.
2. **140 minutes with four full arcs plus Splinter plus Shredder** is six arcs. That is the *Civil War* problem. The remedy the Russos describe (§3.2) is that **arcs collide inside the action**. I will design no subplot that resolves in isolation.
3. **Shredder must not die by accident or from a fall.** Canon has done both (#1). The climax should come from a choice made by our heroes, and that choice should cost them something.
4. **Raphael and Leonardo's conflict is the oldest engine in TMNT** (RTNY). It is a strength, but it risks being predictable. I want a twist on it: the obvious brother to break is Raph, and I think the film is stronger if **Leonardo is the one who breaks**.
5. **The post-credit scene's "green light."** Inside the film, green means mutagen. The audience will connect the two. That is good, as long as the film itself never confirms it.
